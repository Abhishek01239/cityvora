Nairobi is East Africa’s major capital, set at roughly 1,800 metres elevation, combining a modern business skyline, green spaces and the unique experience of a national park on the city’s edge. A two- to four-day visit covers key wildlife attractions, city neighbourhoods and local food while serving as a natural gateway to wider Kenyan safaris.

## Places to Explore

- **Nairobi National Park** — Kenya’s first national park (gazetted 1946), covering about 117 km². It is the only capital-city national park where you can see lions, black rhinos, giraffes, zebras and buffalo with the city skyline in the background. No resident wild elephants. Gates generally open around 6:00 and close around 18:00. Entry for non-resident adults is typically around USD 80 (confirm current rates on the KWS eCitizen portal); pay online in advance as cash is not accepted at gates. Vehicle entry is separate. Best early morning.
- **Sheldrick Wildlife Trust (Elephant Nursery)** — Public viewing of orphaned baby elephants (and often rhinos) during the late-morning feeding session, usually 11:00–12:00. Booking is required and fills up; check the official site for current access. Located near the park.
- **Giraffe Centre** — Elevated platforms where visitors can feed endangered Rothschild’s giraffes. Entry is modest (around KES 1,500 for non-residents; confirm). Good family stop in the Karen/Langata area.
- **Nairobi National Museum** — Strong collections on human origins, Kenyan history and natural heritage. Useful rainy-day option near the centre.
- **Karura Forest** — Large urban forest with walking and cycling trails, waterfalls and good birdlife. Popular local escape.
- **City centre landmarks** — Kenyatta International Convention Centre (KICC) for views, Uhuru Park and nearby gardens, City Market.

Group the park, Sheldrick and Giraffe Centre on the same day in the south-west (Langata/Karen) to minimise traffic. Many visitors also base a short stop here before heading to the Maasai Mara, Amboseli or the coast.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Kenyan food is hearty and centred on grilled meats, maize staples and Indian-Ocean and Swahili influences.

- **Nyama choma** — Grilled goat or beef, ordered by weight and shared. A national favourite; try local grills in Kilimani or Westlands rather than only tourist spots.
- **Ugali and sukuma wiki** — Stiff maize porridge with braised greens; everyday staple.
- **Tilapia and chapati** — Freshwater fish and flatbreads are common.
- **Carnivore Restaurant** — Famous all-you-can-eat meat experience near the park; more tourist-oriented but iconic for first visitors.
- **Coffee** — Strong local coffee culture; many cafés in Westlands, Kilimani and Lavington.

English and Swahili are widely used. M-Pesa mobile money is the dominant payment method; cards work in most hotels and larger restaurants.

## Where to Stay

- **Westlands** — Business and dining hub with many hotels, restaurants and nightlife; practical for first-time visitors.
- **Kilimani / Kileleshwa** — Popular residential-commercial area with cafés, apartments and good access.
- **Karen / Langata** — Leafy suburbs close to the national park, Giraffe Centre and Sheldrick; quieter and greener.
- **Gigiri / Runda** — Near UN offices and embassies; more residential and upscale.

Traffic is heavy; ride-hailing apps (Uber, Bolt, Little) are the easiest way around. Avoid walking after dark in most areas.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights and major safaris (2026 figures; prices vary):

- **Budget:** USD 40–70 — simple guesthouse or hostel, local meals, public transport or shared rides, free or low-cost parks and museums.
- **Mid-range:** USD 80–150 — mid-range hotel, mix of local and restaurant meals, one or two wildlife attractions (park entry is the main cost), ride-hailing.
- **Comfort:** USD 180+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dining, guided park visit and experiences.

Park entry for non-residents is the largest single cost; book and pay on the official KWS portal. M-Pesa or cards cover most other expenses.

## Practical Tips

- Elevation keeps temperatures pleasant year-round; pack a light layer for evenings. Rainy seasons can make park tracks muddy.
- Pay park fees online before arrival. Confirm Sheldrick booking well ahead.
- The city is generally safe in visitor areas (Westlands, Karen, CBD by day) with normal urban awareness. Use ride-hailing at night; do not walk after dark.
- English is widely spoken. Tap water is not always recommended; bottled water is easy to find.
- Nairobi is an excellent short stop or base before longer Kenyan itineraries.

## Conclusion

Nairobi stands out as a capital where you can see wild animals against a skyline and then return to a modern city for dinner. Prioritise an early game drive in Nairobi National Park, add the elephant nursery or Giraffe Centre if timing allows, and spend an evening in Westlands or Karen. With straightforward ride-hailing, strong food options and its role as a safari gateway, the city rewards a deliberate couple of days before or after the wider wilderness.
