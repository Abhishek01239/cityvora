Edinburgh, Scotland’s capital, rises between an extinct volcano and the Firth of Forth, with a medieval Old Town, elegant Georgian New Town and one of Europe’s most dramatic castle skylines. A three- to four-day visit covers the major landmarks, neighbourhood walks and local food without a car.

## Places to Explore

- **Edinburgh Castle** — The fortress on Castle Rock dominates the city. Inside are the Honours of Scotland (crown jewels), the Stone of Destiny and St Margaret’s Chapel, the oldest surviving building in Edinburgh. Adult tickets are around £23.50 when booked online (higher on the day); timed entry is essential in peak season. Allow 2–3 hours. The One O’Clock Gun fires daily except Sundays.
- **The Royal Mile** — The historic spine linking the Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse. Lined with closes, shops, St Giles’ Cathedral (admission charged for non-Scottish visitors) and atmospheric side streets. Free to walk; best early or late to avoid crowds.
- **Arthur’s Seat and Holyrood Park** — An extinct volcano offering free panoramic walks. The summit is 251 m; a popular route from Holyrood takes about 45 minutes up. Stunning views and a genuine outdoor escape in the city.
- **Calton Hill** — Free viewpoint with the National Monument and other neoclassical structures. Excellent for sunset photos of the Castle and Old Town.
- **National Museum of Scotland** — Free, excellent collections covering natural history, Scottish history and world cultures. A good indoor option.
- **Palace of Holyroodhouse** — The King’s official residence in Scotland. State apartments and abbey ruins; tickets around £22 in advance. Closed when the King is in residence—check dates.
- **Other notes** — Dean Village for a picturesque riverside walk, Stockbridge for independent shops, Leith for the port and Royal Yacht Britannia.

Many attractions use timed tickets. The centre is walkable; hills and cobbles reward good shoes.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Scottish food is hearty and flavourful, with excellent seafood, whisky and modern restaurants.

- **Haggis with neeps and tatties** — The classic spiced offal pudding served with mashed turnip and potato. Widely available; vegetarian versions exist.
- **Cullen skink** — Creamy smoked haddock and potato soup.
- **Scotch pies and fish suppers** — Bakery pies and classic fish and chips. Markets and casual spots offer good value.
- **Whisky** — Tastings and bars throughout; the Scotch Whisky Experience near the Castle is popular.
- **Other** — Full Scottish breakfasts, Aberdeen Angus beef, and strong cafe culture. Edinburgh Farmers’ Market (Saturday, Castle Terrace) for local produce.

Tipping around 10% is common if service is not included. Cards are widely accepted.

## Where to Stay

- **Old Town** — Closest to the Castle and Royal Mile; atmospheric but busier and noisier in peak season.
- **New Town** — Georgian elegance, quieter streets, good restaurants and short walks to the centre.
- **Stockbridge / Dean Village** — Residential feel with cafes and easy access.
- **Leith** — Port area with character and dining; a short bus or tram ride to the centre.

Book early for the Edinburgh Festival (August) and summer peak.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season):

- **Budget:** £60–£90 — hostel or simple guesthouse, casual meals, public transport, free sights and one paid attraction.
- **Mid-range:** £120–£200 — mid-range hotel, mixed meals, several paid sights and local transport.
- **Comfort:** £250+ — nicer hotel, restaurant dining and experiences.

Buses and trams are efficient; contactless payment or a day ticket works well. Walking covers most of the centre. A 5% visitor levy applies to accommodation from July 2026.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and daylight is May–September. August is the Festival (busy and atmospheric). Winters are short, cool and dark; Northern Lights are rare but possible further north.
- English is the language. Scots and Gaelic appear in signs and culture.
- Book Castle and popular sites online in advance. Check opening status for royal residences.
- Wear layers and waterproofs; weather changes quickly. Cobblestones and hills demand sturdy shoes.
- Tap water is excellent. The city is generally safe; standard caution in busy areas applies.

## Conclusion

Edinburgh combines dramatic history, compact walkable neighbourhoods and strong food in a setting unlike any other European capital. Prioritise the Castle and Royal Mile, add a walk up Arthur’s Seat or Calton Hill for views, and leave time for local meals. With good public transport and options across budgets, it is straightforward to explore while remaining richly layered.
