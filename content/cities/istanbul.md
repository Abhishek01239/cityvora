Istanbul, straddling Europe and Asia across the Bosphorus, is a layered city of Byzantine and Ottoman history, lively bazaars, waterfront neighborhoods and excellent food. A 3–4 day visit covers the historic peninsula highlights while leaving time for a ferry ride and neighborhood walks.

## Places to Explore

- **Hagia Sophia** — Built in 537 as a cathedral, later a mosque, briefly a museum, and a mosque again since 2020. Free entry for the main hall (dress modestly: cover shoulders and knees; women bring a scarf). Upper gallery and mosaics may have separate fees; go early or late to avoid peak crowds.
- **Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque)** — Directly opposite Hagia Sophia, famous for its six minarets and Iznik tiles. Free outside prayer times; same modest dress code. Closed to tourists during prayers.
- **Topkapi Palace** — Former residence of Ottoman sultans for centuries. Allow several hours; the Harem is a recommended add-on. Check current entry fees (they rise with inflation) and consider a Museum Pass for multiple sites.
- **Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı)** — Vast covered market with thousands of shops under vaulted ceilings. Free to enter; bargaining is expected. Closed Sundays. Nearby Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı) is better for food souvenirs.
- **Basilica Cistern** — Atmospheric underground Byzantine cistern with columns and water. Timed tickets recommended.
- **Bosphorus** — Ferry rides between Eminönü, Karaköy, Beşiktaş and the Asian side (Kadıköy) are both transport and sightseeing. Views of palaces, mosques and the strait.

Other spots: Galata Tower, Istiklal Avenue, and the Asian side for a different pace.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Turkish cuisine is a highlight.

- **Street and casual** — Simit (sesame bread rings), döner, grilled fish sandwiches by the water, baklava and Turkish delight. Spice Bazaar area for sampling.
- **Meze and kebabs** — Shared small plates, grilled meats. Neighborhood restaurants away from pure tourist strips offer better value.
- **Tea and coffee** — Turkish tea is ubiquitous; strong Turkish coffee or modern cafes in Karaköy and Beşiktaş.
- **Markets** — Fresh produce, spices and sweets at the Spice Bazaar or local markets.

Dinner is social; many places open late. No tipping required, though small change is appreciated.

## Where to Stay

- **Sultanahmet** — Walkable to major historic sights; atmospheric but touristy and hilly.
- **Karaköy / Galata** — Trendy, good cafes, close to ferries and Galata Tower; more contemporary vibe.
- **Beyoğlu / Taksim** — Lively, near Istiklal Avenue, nightlife and shopping; funicular connects to the waterfront.
- **Asian side (Kadıköy)** — Local feel, excellent food scene, ferry access; good for longer stays.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices in TRY fluctuate with inflation; confirm current rates):

- **Budget:** ₺1,500–₺2,500 — simple hotel or hostel, street food and casual meals, Istanbulkart, mostly free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** ₺3,000–₺5,000 — mid-range hotel, restaurant meals, paid attractions and ferries.
- **Comfort:** ₺6,000+ — nicer hotel, higher-end dining, taxis or tours.

Get an **Istanbulkart** on arrival for trams, metro, buses, funiculars and ferries (cheaper than single tickets or cash). Transfers are discounted. Taxis are available but use apps like BiTaksi for reliability; traffic can be heavy.

## Practical Tips

- Best seasons: spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) for pleasant weather. Summers are hot; winters mild but rainy.
- Modest dress for mosques. Remove shoes where required.
- Be aware of common tourist approaches (overly friendly shoe-shine or “cousin shop” invitations); decline politely.
- The historic peninsula is walkable but hilly. Use the T1 tram for Sultanahmet–Eminönü and ferries for cross-strait.
- Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for markets and small vendors. ATMs are common.

## Conclusion

Istanbul offers a unique blend of continents, empires and everyday life. Prioritise Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and a Bosphorus ferry, then wander the bazaars or a neighborhood café. With good public transport and layered history, the city rewards both structured sightseeing and spontaneous exploration.
