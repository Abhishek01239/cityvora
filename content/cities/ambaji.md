Ambaji is a temple town in Banaskantha, at the Gujarat edge of the Aravalli hills. Most visits centre on the Arasuri Ambaji Temple, one of the Shakti Peetha sites, and the short trip up to Gabbar Hill.

## Places to Explore

- **Arasuri Ambaji Temple** — the main shrine in town; expect queues on weekends, Navratri and full-moon days.
- **Gabbar Hill** — hilltop temple linked with the Ambaji tradition; a ropeway runs when it is operating, and the road route is the backup.
- **Ambaji town market** — shops for prasad, religious items and simple local snacks.
- **Balaram Ambaji Wildlife Sanctuary** — nearby forest area for a quieter add-on if you have a vehicle.
- **Abu Road / Mount Abu** — Rajasthan side of the hills, often paired with Ambaji on a longer trip.

## Food & Local Food Areas

- **Temple approach:** Simple Gujarati meals, tea and snacks aimed at pilgrims.
- **Town market:** Fafda, gathiya, sweets and thali restaurants.
- **Bus stand side:** Practical stops before an early temple visit.

Stall names and timings can change, so check the current vendor location locally. Many eateries are vegetarian.

## Where to Stay

- **Ambaji town:** Dharamshalas, guest houses and small hotels within walking distance of the temple.
- **Abu Road:** More rooms if Ambaji is full on festival nights, a short drive across the state border.
- **Palanpur:** Wider hotel choice, about 1.5–2 hours by road, if you want a quieter base.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding flights:

- **Budget:** ₹800–₹2,000
- **Mid-range:** ₹2,200–₹4,500
- **Comfort/premium:** ₹5,000+
- **Food:** roughly ₹250–₹900/day.
- **Local transport:** roughly ₹100–₹800/day, more if you add the Gabbar ropeway or a cab to the sanctuary.

Hotel prices jump during Navratri and other festival dates; check live rates before booking.

## Two-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Ambaji Temple → town market → evening aarti if timings allow.

**Day 2:** Gabbar Hill in the morning → optional Balaram sanctuary or onward to Mount Abu.

## Before You Go

- October to March is generally more comfortable, but festival days are crowded.
- Dress modestly and follow temple rules on phones, leather and queues.
- Confirm ropeway status before planning Gabbar only around it.
- Keep a buffer if you are travelling during Navratri.
