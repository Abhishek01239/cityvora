Singapore is a compact, highly efficient city-state where modern skyscrapers meet preserved shophouses, multicultural neighborhoods and world-class street food. A three- to four-day visit easily covers the iconic Marina Bay landmarks, a couple of historic districts and the essential hawker-centre experience, all connected by a clean, reliable MRT system.

## Places to Explore

- **Marina Bay Sands and Marina Bay** — The three-tower hotel with its skypark is the city’s defining modern landmark. The observation deck is open to the public (timed tickets recommended); the rooftop infinity pool is for hotel guests only. Walk the waterfront promenade for free views of the skyline and the Merlion.
- **Gardens by the Bay** — Supertree Grove is free to visit and especially atmospheric during the evening light show. The Flower Dome and Cloud Forest conservatories require paid tickets and are best visited earlier in the day. The outdoor gardens offer shaded paths and city views.
- **Chinatown** — Colorful shophouses, the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, Sri Mariamman Temple and Maxwell Food Centre. Walkable and atmospheric; early mornings or evenings are quieter.
- **Little India and Kampong Glam** — Vibrant Indian quarter with Tekka Centre and spice shops; Kampong Glam features the Sultan Mosque and Arab Street for a different cultural layer.
- **Singapore Botanic Gardens** — UNESCO-listed free gardens with the National Orchid Garden (paid) as a highlight. A pleasant contrast to the urban core.
- **Sentosa Island** — Beaches, Universal Studios and cable-car views; accessible by MRT plus a short monorail or walk.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Singapore’s greatest everyday luxury is its hawker centres — open-air food courts with dozens of specialist stalls. They are inexpensive, diverse and UNESCO-recognized as intangible cultural heritage.

- **Hainanese chicken rice** — The national dish. Maxwell Food Centre (Tian Tian) and many others serve excellent versions for a few dollars.
- **Laksa, char kway teow, satay and chili crab** — Coconut-based noodle soup, wok-fried noodles, grilled skewers and the signature crab dish (more expensive even at hawkers).
- **Hawker centres to try** — Maxwell, Lau Pa Sat (evening satay street), Chinatown Complex, Old Airport Road and Tiong Bahru Market.
- **Other options** — Kopi and kaya toast at traditional coffee shops, Peranakan (Nyonya) cuisine in Katong/Joo Chiat, and higher-end dining in the CBD or Marina Bay.

A full hawker meal typically costs S$4–S$10. Tap water is safe; tipping is not expected at hawkers.

## Where to Stay

- **Chinatown / Tanjong Pagar** — Central, walkable to food and MRT, good mix of hotels and hostels.
- **Marina Bay / Civic District** — Closest to the headline landmarks; higher rates.
- **Orchard Road** — Shopping and convenient MRT connections.
- **Little India / Kampong Glam** — More budget-friendly and atmospheric.

The MRT makes almost any central base workable.

**Hotel booking:** Search Singapore hotels on major platforms; rates rise during major events and peak holiday periods.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; confirm current rates):

- **Budget:** S$80–S$120 — hostel dorm, hawker meals, MRT, free attractions.
- **Mid-range:** S$200–S$350 — comfortable hotel, mix of hawker and restaurant meals, paid sights.
- **Comfort:** S$400+ — higher-end hotel, fine dining, taxis or premium experiences.

MRT adult card fares range from about S$1.28 to S$2.57 depending on distance. Contactless bank cards or the SimplyGo app work directly at gates (foreign cards may incur a small daily fee). An EZ-Link card is an alternative for cash users.

## Practical Tips

- The MRT is the easiest way to get around; stations are clean, air-conditioned and well-signed. Use Google Maps or the official app for routing.
- Best weather is relatively drier months (roughly February–April and July–September); the city is warm and humid year-round with possible afternoon showers.
- English is widely spoken. The city is extremely safe and clean.
- Book popular attractions such as the Gardens conservatories or Marina Bay Sands SkyPark in advance during busy periods.
- Carry a light layer for air-conditioned indoor spaces.

## Conclusion

Singapore packs impressive density of landmarks, cultures and food into a small, easy-to-navigate space. Prioritise Gardens by the Bay and the Marina Bay waterfront, then spend time in Chinatown or Little India eating at hawker centres. With excellent public transport and options at every budget, it is one of the most straightforward major cities in Asia to explore.
