Melbourne is the capital of the state of Victoria and Australia’s second-largest city. It is known for its laneway culture, strong coffee scene, multicultural food, parks and a mix of Victorian-era buildings with modern architecture.

## Places to Explore

- **CBD Laneways** — Hosier Lane (street art), Degraves Street and Centre Place for cafés and atmosphere. The free tram zone covers much of the central grid.
- **Queen Victoria Market** — Historic market for produce, food stalls and shopping. Check opening days (closed Mondays and Wednesdays for some sections).
- **Royal Botanic Gardens and Shrine of Remembrance** — Large gardens along the Yarra River and the nearby memorial. Good for walking and views.
- **Federation Square and NGV** — Cultural hub by the river; the National Gallery of Victoria (NGV International) is free for the permanent collection.
- **St Kilda** — Beach suburb with the pier, Luna Park and a lively food strip on Acland Street. Reachable by tram.
- **Fitzroy and Collingwood** — Inner-north neighborhoods with street art, independent shops, cafés and restaurants on Brunswick Street and Smith Street.

The city center is walkable; trams make outer neighborhoods easy to reach.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Melbourne is one of Australia’s best food cities, with strong influences from Italian, Greek, Vietnamese, Chinese and other communities.

- **Coffee** — Flat white or the local “magic” (double ristretto with milk). Independent cafés are preferred over chains.
- **Multicultural options** — Excellent Italian in Carlton (Lygon Street), Vietnamese in Richmond, Chinese in Chinatown (Little Bourke Street), and many others.
- **Markets and casual** — Queen Victoria Market for fresh food and stalls; laneway cafés for breakfast or lunch.
- **Neighborhood dining** — Fitzroy, Collingwood and Prahran offer independent restaurants and wine bars.

Tipping is not expected; a small amount is appreciated for good service.

## Where to Stay

- **CBD / Southbank** — Most convenient for first visits, walkable to major sights and the free tram zone.
- **Fitzroy / Collingwood** — Better for food, nightlife and a local feel; short tram or train ride to the center.
- **St Kilda** — Beach atmosphere and good for longer stays or those wanting sea air.
- **South Yarra / Prahran** — Shopping, cafés and a residential vibe with good transport.

Accommodation ranges from hostels to boutique hotels. Book ahead for major events or peak seasons.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary):

- **Budget:** A$80–A$140 — hostel or simple room, market or café meals, public transport, free attractions.
- **Mid-range:** A$180–A$300 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, some paid experiences and transport.
- **Comfort:** A$350+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and guided activities.

Trams in the free zone cost nothing; a myki card covers the wider network. Many parks, laneways and the NGV permanent collection are free.

## Practical Tips

- Weather changes quickly (“four seasons in one day”). Carry a light layer.
- Best times are autumn (March–May) and spring (September–November) for milder weather and fewer extremes.
- Public transport is reliable; download the PTV app or use contactless payment where available.
- English is the language. The city is generally safe; use normal urban caution at night.
- Coffee culture is serious — good independent cafés are everywhere.

## Conclusion

Melbourne rewards time spent in its laneways, parks and neighborhoods rather than a single landmark checklist. A three- to four-day visit covers the center, a market, a garden or beach, and at least one outer neighborhood for food. With excellent public transport, strong coffee and diverse dining, the city is easy to enjoy at a relaxed pace.
