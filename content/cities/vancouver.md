Vancouver sits on the Pacific coast of British Columbia, wedged between Burrard Inlet, the Fraser River and the Coast Mountains. The city proper has roughly 662,000 residents (2021 census); Metro Vancouver is closer to 2.6–3 million. Nature is immediate: a large urban rainforest park, a continuous seawall, beaches and mountain trails are all reachable without a car.

A focused three- to five-day visit covers Stanley Park, waterfront neighborhoods and at least one North Shore excursion.

## Places to Explore

- **Stanley Park** — One of North America’s largest urban parks (about 400 hectares), with temperate rainforest, beaches and the Seawall path. The full Seawall loop is roughly 9–10 km and is popular for walking or cycling. Totem poles at Brockton Point, Lost Lagoon and viewpoints toward the Lions Gate Bridge and downtown are free. Bike rentals are widely available near the park entrances.
- **Canada Place and Coal Harbour** — The white sail-roofed convention centre is a classic waterfront landmark. Walk the seawall for views of cruise ships, floatplanes and the North Shore mountains.
- **Gastown** — Vancouver’s oldest neighborhood, a National Historic Site. Cobblestone Water Street, Victorian buildings and the Steam Clock (which whistles every 15 minutes) form the core. Good for independent shops and evening dining.
- **Granville Island** — Public market, artisan studios and waterfront stalls. Reachable by short Aquabus or False Creek ferry from the downtown side. Excellent for lunch and browsing.
- **Chinatown and Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Classical Chinese Garden** — Adjacent to Gastown; the classical garden is a calm, ticketed highlight.
- **North Shore options** — Capilano Suspension Bridge Park (paid, book ahead) or free Lynn Canyon for rainforest walks and bridges. Grouse Mountain offers gondola rides and views (paid).

The historic core and waterfront are walkable or bikeable. Group sights by area to minimize transit time.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Vancouver has a strong multicultural food scene, especially Asian (Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese) alongside Pacific Northwest seafood and a serious coffee culture.

- **Granville Island Public Market** — Fresh produce, seafood, prepared foods and bakeries. Ideal for a casual lunch or picnic ingredients.
- **Seafood** — BC salmon, oysters and other local catch appear on menus across the city.
- **Asian neighborhoods** — Chinatown and nearby areas for dim sum and Chinese; Richmond (a short SkyTrain ride) is known for excellent Chinese restaurants and night markets.
- **Neighborhood dining** — Gastown, Yaletown and Main Street (Mount Pleasant) for independent restaurants and breweries. Kitsilano has casual beach-adjacent options.
- **Coffee** — Independent cafés are the norm; a flat white or similar is widely available.

Tipping 15–20% is expected in restaurants and for good service. Cards and contactless payment work almost everywhere.

## Where to Stay

- **Downtown / Coal Harbour / West End** — Most convenient for first visits, walkable to Stanley Park, seawall and major sights. Higher rates.
- **Yaletown / Gastown** — Converted warehouses, dining and nightlife; still central.
- **Kitsilano** — Beach and neighborhood feel; short transit or bike ride to the center.
- **Near SkyTrain stations outside the core** — Better value in Burnaby or Richmond while remaining connected.

Book early for summer (June–September) and major events. FIFA World Cup 2026 matches in the city will increase demand that summer.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and exact choices):

- **Budget:** C$100–C$160 — hostel or simple hotel, market or casual meals, transit, free parks and seawall.
- **Mid-range:** C$200–C$350 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, one or two paid attractions, local transport.
- **Comfort:** C$400+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and premium experiences.

TransLink fares (as of mid-2026): adult one-zone about C$3.50 cash/tap or lower with a Compass Card; DayPass around C$12.55. The Canada Line from the airport to downtown takes about 25–30 minutes. Many parks, beaches and exterior walks are free.

## Practical Tips

- Best overall weather is June–September (drier and milder). May and September offer a good balance of conditions and fewer peak crowds. October–April is wetter and cooler in the city but better for nearby skiing and lower hotel rates.
- Pack layers and a rain jacket year-round; evenings on the water can be cool even in summer.
- English is the primary language. Tap water is safe. Cards work widely; carry a little cash for small stalls.
- Transit is efficient (SkyTrain, buses, SeaBus). Contactless bank cards or a Compass Card work at gates. Avoid peak rush hours when possible.
- The city is generally safe. The Downtown Eastside near parts of Gastown/Chinatown has visible social challenges; stick to main streets after dark and treat it as a residential area rather than a sightseeing stop.
- Book popular paid attractions (Capilano, Grouse, some museums) online in advance during busy periods.

## Conclusion

Vancouver rewards time spent outdoors as much as classic urban sightseeing. Prioritize Stanley Park and the Seawall, add Granville Island and Gastown, then choose one North Shore excursion. With strong public transit, free natural spaces and excellent food options across budgets, the city is straightforward to explore while remaining defined by its mountains, water and parks.
