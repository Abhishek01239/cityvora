London is the UK’s capital and one of the world’s most visited cities, layering centuries of history, royal landmarks, world-class free museums, diverse neighborhoods and a strong food scene along the Thames. A focused four- to five-day visit covers the classic sights while leaving time for neighborhood walks and markets.

## Places to Explore

- **Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben) and Palace of Westminster** — The clock tower and Houses of Parliament form the classic Thames view. Exterior photography is free from Westminster Bridge or the South Bank; interior tours of Parliament require advance booking.
- **Tower of London** — Nearly 1,000-year-old fortress that has served as palace, prison and treasury. Highlights include the Crown Jewels, White Tower and Yeoman Warder tours. Book timed tickets online, especially in peak season.
- **British Museum** — Free entry to one of the world’s great collections (Rosetta Stone, Egyptian antiquities and more). Allow at least half a day; it is closed on some Mondays or check current hours.
- **Westminster Abbey** — Gothic royal church and coronation site. Paid entry; book ahead. Nearby St. James’s Park offers a pleasant walk toward Buckingham Palace.
- **Buckingham Palace** — Official royal residence. The State Rooms are open to visitors in summer; the Changing of the Guard ceremony (when it runs) is free to watch from outside.
- **South Bank walk** — From Westminster Bridge toward Tower Bridge: London Eye, Southbank Centre, Tate Modern (free), Shakespeare’s Globe and Borough Market. Excellent for free views and atmosphere.

Other strong free options: National Gallery, Tate Britain, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Hyde Park and the Tower Bridge exterior.

## Food & Local Cuisine

London’s food is global and excellent at every budget.

- **Borough Market** — Historic food market (best Thursday–Saturday) for cheeses, breads, oysters, sandwiches and prepared foods. Arrive earlier to avoid peak crowds.
- **Fish and chips, pie and mash, Sunday roast** — Classic British options available widely; look for well-reviewed local pubs or chippies away from pure tourist strips.
- **International depth** — Indian (Brick Lane area), Chinese (Chinatown), Middle Eastern, Japanese and more. Hawker-style or market stalls offer good value.
- **Markets and casual** — Spitalfields, Maltby Street or neighborhood cafés for coffee, pastries and light meals. Afternoon tea is a popular (and often pricey) experience.

Service charge is often included in restaurants; tipping extra is optional.

## Where to Stay

- **Covent Garden / Soho / West End** — Central for theatres, restaurants and walking to many sights; higher rates.
- **South Bank / Bankside** — Near the river, Tate Modern and Borough Market; good transport links.
- **Westminster** — Close to the big landmarks.
- **Shoreditch / Spitalfields or Camden** — More neighborhood character, markets and slightly better value; short Tube or bus rides to the center.

Zone 1 or well-connected Zone 2 bases work best for first visits. Use major booking platforms and check cancellation policies.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and exact choices):

- **Budget:** £70–£110 — hostel or simple hotel, market/café meals, contactless Tube/bus (daily cap), free museums plus one paid sight.
- **Mid-range:** £150–£250 — mid-range hotel, mix of casual and restaurant meals, several attractions or a theatre ticket, local transport.
- **Comfort:** £300+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners, taxis or premium experiences.

Contactless cards or phones work directly on Tube and buses (daily cap applies in Zones 1–2). Many major museums are free. Book popular paid sites (Tower of London, London Eye, Westminster Abbey) online in advance.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and crowds is late spring and early autumn. Summers are warmer and busier; winters are cooler, darker and cheaper with fewer visitors.
- The Tube, buses and walking cover most needs. Use Citymapper or Google Maps for routing. Avoid peak rush hours when possible.
- Book timed-entry attractions ahead. Many free museums recommend or require advance reservation in busy periods.
- Tap water is safe. English is the language. The city is generally safe; watch belongings in crowded tourist areas.
- Carry a light waterproof layer year-round.

## Conclusion

London rewards a mix of landmark visits and neighborhood time. Prioritise the Thames landmarks and a couple of free museums, then explore Covent Garden, the South Bank or an East London market. With excellent public transport, abundant free culture and options at every budget, the city is straightforward to navigate while remaining richly layered.
