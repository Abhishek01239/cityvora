New York City spans five boroughs and packs dense neighborhoods, world-famous landmarks, and exceptional food into a compact, walkable core linked by an extensive subway. A four- to seven-day visit covers the major sights while leaving room for neighborhood exploration.

## Places to Explore

- **Central Park** — 843-acre public park in the center of Manhattan with paths, lakes, bridges and lawns. Free entry; popular for walks, picnics and seasonal events. Pair with nearby museums such as the Metropolitan Museum of Art or American Museum of Natural History.
- **Brooklyn Bridge** — Iconic suspension bridge with a pedestrian walkway offering skyline views. Free; best early morning or at dusk to manage crowds. Combine with a walk in DUMBO or Brooklyn Bridge Park.
- **Times Square and Midtown** — Bright commercial intersection and theater district. Free to visit; useful as a base for Broadway, Rockefeller Center and nearby attractions.
- **Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island** — Accessible by ferry from Battery Park or New Jersey. Reserve timed tickets in advance; the pedestal or crown experiences sell out.
- **Empire State Building or Top of the Rock** — Observation decks with city views. Tickets typically start around $40–60 depending on level and time; book ahead for peak periods.
- **Neighborhood walks** — Greenwich Village, SoHo, Chinatown and the Lower East Side offer distinct character, architecture and street life within easy subway or walking distance.

## Food & Local Cuisine

New York is one of the world’s great food cities, with options from classic street food to high-end restaurants.

- **Iconic everyday foods** — New York-style pizza slices, bagels with cream cheese and lox, pastrami sandwiches (Katz’s Delicatessen is a longstanding reference), and dollar-slice or neighborhood pizzerias.
- **Markets and food halls** — Chelsea Market, Smorgasburg (seasonal), and various depots or neighborhood markets for sampling.
- **Neighborhood cuisines** — Chinatown for Cantonese and regional Chinese, Jackson Heights or Flushing in Queens for South Asian and Chinese, Astoria for Greek and Mediterranean influences.
- **Budget and mid-range** — Food carts, delis, and casual restaurants keep daily food costs manageable; many places accept cards, though some smaller spots prefer cash.

Service is often not included; 15–20% tip is standard in restaurants.

## Where to Stay

- **Midtown Manhattan** — Convenient for first-time visitors, close to Times Square, Broadway and major attractions; higher rates and more crowds.
- **Lower Manhattan / Financial District** — Near the Brooklyn Bridge, Battery Park and Wall Street; good transit links.
- **Brooklyn (Williamsburg, DUMBO, Park Slope)** — More residential atmosphere, often better value, with strong food and nightlife scenes.
- **Upper West Side or near Central Park** — Quieter base with park access and museum proximity.

Rates rise sharply during peak seasons, holidays and major events; book early.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international or domestic flights (prices vary by season and exchange rates):

- **Budget:** $100–180 — hostel or budget hotel, casual and street food, subway rides, free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** $200–350 — mid-range hotel, mixed restaurant meals, a paid attraction or show, local transport.
- **Comfort:** $400+ — higher-end hotel, fine dining, theater tickets and private experiences.

The subway is the main way to move around; contactless payment (OMNY) is widely used. Walking covers many central neighborhoods.

## Practical Tips

- Best weather for outdoor walking is spring (April–June) and fall (September–November). Summers can be hot and humid; winters are cold with possible snow.
- Book timed-entry attractions (Statue of Liberty, observation decks, popular museums) in advance.
- The city is generally safe in tourist areas; use standard urban awareness, especially on the subway at night and in less-familiar neighborhoods.
- English is the primary language. Cards and contactless payments are widely accepted; carry some cash for tips and smaller vendors.
- Subway runs 24 hours on most lines, but service changes occur; check apps for real-time information.

## Conclusion

New York rewards both first-time visitors and repeat travelers with its layered neighborhoods, food diversity and efficient transit. Prioritize a few neighborhoods and landmarks rather than trying to see everything, and leave time for spontaneous walks and meals. With options at every budget and a walkable core, the city is straightforward to navigate while remaining endlessly varied.
