Cairo is Egypt’s capital and one of the world’s great historic cities, home to the Giza Pyramids, dense medieval Islamic quarters and a vibrant street-food culture. A three- to five-day visit covers the ancient sites, Islamic Cairo and some modern neighborhoods while remaining manageable with taxis or ride apps.

## Places to Explore

- **Giza Pyramids and Sphinx** — The last remaining Wonder of the Ancient World. The Great Pyramid of Khufu, Pyramid of Khafre and Menkaure, plus the Great Sphinx. Go early to avoid heat and crowds. Combination tickets or site entry available; check current rules for climbing or interior access.
- **Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)** — Large modern museum near the pyramids housing extensive collections including Tutankhamun artifacts. Timed tickets recommended.
- **Islamic Cairo and Al-Muizz Street** — Historic pedestrian thoroughfare with medieval mosques, madrasas and architecture, floodlit in the evenings. Adjacent to Khan el-Khalili bazaar for traditional shopping and cafés.
- **Coptic Cairo (Old Cairo)** — Compact area with the Hanging Church, Ben Ezra Synagogue and Coptic Museum, reflecting layered religious history.
- **Other** — Nile views from Zamalek or downtown. Cairo Citadel for elevated city and mosque views.

The pyramids and museum make a full day; Islamic Cairo rewards slow walking in the cooler hours.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Egyptian food is filling and inexpensive.

- **Koshary** — National dish of lentils, rice, pasta and crispy onions. Available at dedicated shops and street stalls.
- **Ful and ta’amiya** — Fava-bean stew and Egyptian falafel, common breakfast items.
- **Street food and grills** — Corn, sweet potatoes, sandwiches and simple grilled meats. Khan el-Khalili area has cafés for tea and shisha.
- **Other** — Meze plates, molokhia (green soup), and Nile-view restaurants in Zamalek or higher-end spots with pyramid views.

Street food is generally safe if hot and busy. Bottled water is recommended. Tipping small amounts is common.

## Where to Stay

- **Downtown / Near Tahrir** — Central for museums and Nile access.
- **Zamalek** — Island neighborhood with a more residential feel, cafés and galleries.
- **Near Giza** — Convenient for the pyramids but farther from Islamic Cairo.
- **Islamic Cairo edge** — Atmospheric but can be denser and noisier.

Mid-range hotels and apartments are widely available. Book ahead in peak winter months.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary and EGP fluctuates):

- **Budget:** $25–45 — simple hotel or hostel, street food and local meals, public transport or shared taxis, site entry.
- **Mid-range:** $50–90 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, private or small-group transport for pyramids, paid museum entry.
- **Comfort:** $120+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dining and guided experiences.

Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Careem) are practical. Metro exists but coverage is limited for tourist sites. Cash is essential for smaller purchases.

## Practical Tips

- Cooler months (October–April) are most comfortable. Summers are very hot.
- Dress modestly, especially when visiting mosques (shoulders and knees covered; women may need a headscarf for some sites).
- Cairo is generally safe for tourists with normal big-city awareness. Use reputable transport.
- Book major sites and the museum in advance where possible. Early starts help with heat and crowds.
- English is spoken in tourist areas. Google Maps works; local SIM or eSIM is useful.

## Conclusion

Cairo combines ancient monuments on a global scale with a dense, living historic core. Prioritize the Giza Plateau and Grand Egyptian Museum for the pyramids experience, then explore Al-Muizz Street and Khan el-Khalili for Islamic Cairo atmosphere. With affordable food, practical transport options and layered history, the city rewards both short focused visits and longer stays centered on its neighborhoods and cuisine.
