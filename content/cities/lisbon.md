Lisbon rises across seven hills above the Tagus River, mixing Moorish lanes, Manueline monuments from the Age of Discovery, yellow trams and a thriving food scene. A three- to four-day visit covers the historic centre, Belém and local neighbourhoods without needing a car.

## Places to Explore

- **Castelo de São Jorge** — The hilltop castle overlooking Alfama. Walls and towers give the best panoramic views of the red rooftops and river. Adult tickets are around €17; go early or late to avoid queues. Allow 1.5–2 hours.
- **Alfama** — The oldest neighbourhood, with winding streets, tiled houses, miradouros (viewpoints) and fado houses. Free to wander; best explored on foot or by the classic Tram 28.
- **Belém** — Home to the UNESCO-listed Jerónimos Monastery (Manueline cloisters and Vasco da Gama’s tomb; timed tickets around €18) and the Belém Tower by the river. Pair with the Monument to the Discoveries and Pastéis de Belém.
- **Praça do Comércio and Baixa** — The grand riverfront square rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake, with the Arco da Rua Augusta. Free to visit; ideal for a stroll.
- **Tram 28** — The iconic yellow tram through Alfama, Baixa and Estrela. Buy a ticket onboard or use a transport card; expect crowds and pickpocket risk.
- **Other notes** — Miradouro da Senhora do Monte or Santa Luzia for free sunset views; LX Factory for creative shops and food; day trip to Sintra for palaces.

The centre is walkable but hilly with slippery calcada cobbles—wear good shoes. Metro, buses and trams are efficient.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Portuguese food is hearty, with excellent seafood, grilled meats and pastries.

- **Pastéis de nata** — Custard tarts. The original Pastéis de Belém has baked them since 1837; Manteigaria in Chiado serves them warm with a bell for fresh batches; neighbourhood spots like Aloma are local favourites. Around €1.30–1.60 each.
- **Seafood** — Grilled sardines (especially in June), bacalhau (salt cod) in many styles, and fresh fish.
- **Other** — Bifanas (pork sandwiches), ginjinha (cherry liqueur), and strong coffee culture (bica = espresso).
- **Fado** — Traditional melancholic song, best in small Alfama houses in the evening.

Tipping 5–10% is common if service is not included. Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for small cafes and markets.

## Where to Stay

- **Baixa / Chiado** — Central, flat-ish and close to transport and sights.
- **Alfama / Graça** — Atmospheric and historic; steeper streets and more character.
- **Príncipe Real / Bairro Alto** — Trendy dining and nightlife; easy access to the centre.
- **Belém** — Quieter riverside base if monuments are the priority.

Book early for summer and festival periods.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season):

- **Budget:** €50–€80 — hostel or simple guesthouse, casual meals, public transport, free sights and one paid attraction.
- **Mid-range:** €100–€180 — mid-range hotel, mixed meals, several paid sights and local transport.
- **Comfort:** €200+ — nicer hotel, restaurant dining and experiences.

A reusable transport card (Navegante) keeps costs down. A tourist tax of around €4 per adult per night applies.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and crowds is April–June or September–October. Summers are hot and busy; winters are mild but wetter. June brings the Santo António festival with street parties.
- Portuguese is the language; English is widely spoken in tourist areas.
- Book Jerónimos and popular sites online in advance. Decline Dynamic Currency Conversion at ATMs and always use Multibanco bank machines.
- Wear sturdy shoes for hills and cobbles. Tap water is safe. The city is generally safe; watch belongings on trams and in crowds.

## Conclusion

Lisbon combines dramatic hilltop views, living history from the Age of Discovery, compact walkable (if hilly) neighbourhoods and outstanding food in a sunny riverside setting. Prioritise the castle and Alfama for atmosphere, Belém for monuments, and leave time for warm pastéis de nata and a tram ride. With good public transport and options across budgets, it is easy to explore while feeling richly layered.
