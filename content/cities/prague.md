Prague, the capital of Czechia, is one of Europe’s most intact historic cities, with a hilltop castle complex, a statue-lined bridge over the Vltava, a compact Old Town and a strong beer-and-pub culture. A three- to four-day visit covers the main landmarks while leaving time for neighborhood walks and meals.

## Places to Explore

- **Prague Castle** — Large complex on the hill above the river, including St Vitus Cathedral, the Old Royal Palace and Golden Lane. Grounds are free; interiors require tickets. Arrive early. Tram 22 connects from the centre.
- **Charles Bridge** — Gothic stone bridge lined with Baroque statues. Free and open 24 hours. Best at dawn or early morning before crowds.
- **Old Town Square** — Historic square with the Astronomical Clock (Orloj), which performs on the hour, and the Church of Our Lady before Týn. Free to wander; the clock tower offers views.
- **Malá Strana** — Baroque district below the castle with cobbled streets, gardens and the Charles Bridge approach.
- **Josefov (Jewish Quarter)** — Synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery, managed as museum sites. Check current ticket arrangements and closing days.
- **Vyšehrad** — Fortress and park south of the centre with river views and a quieter atmosphere.

The historic core is walkable but hilly and cobbled. Group sights by area (castle side, Old Town, New Town).

## Food & Local Cuisine

Czech food is hearty and good value, especially in neighborhood pubs.

- **Svíčková** — Beef sirloin in creamy root-vegetable sauce with dumplings, a classic.
- **Goulash and pork knuckle** — Common pub dishes served with dumplings or bread.
- **Beer** — Pilsner and other lagers; Czechia has high per-capita consumption. Many pubs serve fresh tank beer.
- **Street and casual** — Trdelník (sweet pastry), open sandwiches (chlebíčky) and bakery items.
- **Dessert** — Apple strudel is widely available.

Eat away from pure tourist strips near Old Town Square for better value. Many places open late.

## Where to Stay

- **Old Town (Staré Město)** — Central for first visits; walkable to major sights but busier and pricier.
- **Malá Strana** — Atmospheric, close to the bridge and castle.
- **Vinohrady** — Leafier, more local feel with good cafés and tram/metro links; often better value.
- **New Town / near Wenceslas Square** — Practical for transport and mid-range hotels.

Public transport makes most central neighborhoods convenient.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; confirm current prices):

- **Budget:** 1,200–1,800 CZK — hostel or simple guesthouse, pub meals, public transport, free sights.
- **Mid-range:** 2,500–4,000 CZK — comfortable hotel, restaurant meals, paid attractions and transport.
- **Comfort:** 5,000+ CZK — higher-end hotel and finer dining.

Public transport (metro, trams, buses) is inexpensive and efficient. A 24-hour pass is good value. Contactless cards work on validators. Carry some cash for smaller pubs. Avoid unofficial exchange booths in the tourist centre.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and crowds: May, June, September and October. July–August is busiest. December has Christmas markets. Winter is cold but quieter.
- Wear comfortable shoes with good grip for cobbles and hills.
- Charles Bridge and many exteriors are free; book popular interiors in advance.
- English is spoken in tourist areas; basic Czech phrases are appreciated.
- The city is very safe. Watch for common tourist exchange scams in the centre.
- Cards are widely accepted; Czech koruna is the currency (not euro).

## Conclusion

Prague rewards walking between its castle, bridge and Old Town while pausing for a local beer and a proper meal. Start early on Charles Bridge and the castle, then explore by neighborhood. With excellent public transport, compact distances and strong value, the city is easy to enjoy while remaining richly historic.
