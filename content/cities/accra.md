Accra is Ghana’s coastal capital, combining independence-era monuments, a lively food and music scene, beach neighborhoods and modern districts. A two- to four-day visit covers key historical sites, local markets and dining while serving as a base for wider Ghana travel.

## Places to Explore

- **Independence Square (Black Star Square) and Black Star Gate** — Large ceremonial square with the iconic gate marked “Freedom and Justice” and the black star. Important for Ghana’s 1957 independence history.
- **Kwame Nkrumah Memorial Park** — Mausoleum and museum dedicated to Ghana’s first president, with statues and fountains in a landscaped setting.
- **Jamestown** — Historic fishing quarter with the red-and-white lighthouse, colonial buildings, boxing gyms and harbor. Best explored with awareness; some guided walks are available.
- **Makola Market** — One of the city’s largest markets for fabrics, food, crafts and everyday goods. Intense and rewarding; keep valuables secure and carry small notes.
- **Labadi Beach** — Popular beach with local activity, especially on weekends. Combine with nearby areas for a coastal feel.
- **National Museum or other cultural sites** — Collections covering Ghanaian history and art; useful for context.

Group the square, Nkrumah Memorial and nearby central areas on one day; Jamestown and market on another.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Ghanaian food is flavorful, centered on rice, stews, grilled fish and plantains.

- **Waakye** — Rice and beans cooked with millet leaves for a reddish color, served with options like fried plantain, egg, spaghetti, shito (chili sauce) and protein. A breakfast or lunch staple found at street stalls and restaurants.
- **Jollof rice** — Tomato-based rice, widely available and a point of regional pride.
- **Banku and grilled tilapia** — Fermented corn dough with fish and pepper sauce; common at chop bars.
- **Kelewele** — Spicy fried plantains, often a street snack.
- **Other** — Fufu with light soup, grilled meats and fresh seafood near the coast.

English is the official language and widely spoken. Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Bolt) are practical; shared trotros (minibuses) offer a local experience but require local knowledge.

## Where to Stay

- **Osu** — Central entertainment and dining area with shops and nightlife; convenient but can be lively at night.
- **East Legon / Airport Residential** — More upscale, secure residential-commercial zones with restaurants and hotels; good for first-time visitors.
- **Labone or nearby** — Mix of residential and commercial with easier access to some attractions.

Traffic can be heavy; choose a base that matches your priorities and use apps for movement after dark.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary):

- **Budget:** USD 40–70 — simple guesthouse or hostel, local meals, shared transport or basic rides, free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** USD 80–150 — mid-range hotel, mix of local and restaurant meals, ride-hailing, one or two attractions.
- **Comfort:** USD 180+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dining and guided experiences.

Mobile money (especially MoMo) and cards work in many places; cash is useful for markets and street food.

## Practical Tips

- The drier months (roughly November–March) are more comfortable for walking. Rainy periods can affect traffic and outdoor plans.
- Accra is generally safe in visitor areas with normal urban caution. Use ride-hailing at night; avoid displaying valuables in crowded markets.
- English is widely used. Tap water is not always recommended; bottled water is easy to find.
- Confirm current hours for memorials and museums. December can be busy with diaspora visitors and events.

## Conclusion

Accra offers a clear window into Ghana’s independence story alongside everyday street food, markets and coastal energy. Prioritize the Black Star Square and Nkrumah Memorial, add Jamestown or Makola for atmosphere, and eat widely. With improving ride-hailing options and a compact set of core sights, the city rewards a deliberate short stay.
