Berlin is Germany’s capital and largest city, a place shaped by 20th-century history yet defined today by creative neighborhoods, excellent museums, and a relaxed, bike-friendly atmosphere. Its central sights are compact enough for walking or short transit rides, while a three-to-five-day visit covers the essentials without rushing.

## Places to Explore

- **Brandenburg Gate** — The neoclassical landmark on Pariser Platz is Berlin’s most recognizable symbol. Free and always open, it sits at the edge of the Tiergarten. Visit early or late for softer light and fewer crowds.
- **Reichstag Building and Glass Dome** — The seat of the German parliament offers free entry to its Norman Foster-designed glass dome, with views over the city and into the debating chamber. Advance online registration (with passport details) is required; book days or weeks ahead via the official Bundestag site. Walk-ins are rarely accepted.
- **Museum Island** — A UNESCO World Heritage site on the Spree River housing five major museums, including the Neues Museum (Egyptian collection and Nefertiti bust) and the Pergamon Museum (currently closed for renovation). The Berlin Cathedral (Berliner Dom) is nearby. A Museum Pass Berlin covers many sites for three consecutive days.
- **East Side Gallery** — The longest remaining section of the Berlin Wall, painted with large-scale murals (including the famous Fraternal Kiss). Free to visit along Mühlenstraße in Friedrichshain; go early to avoid tour groups.
- **Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe** — A field of concrete stelae just south of the Brandenburg Gate. Free, open daily, and best experienced by walking through the undulating ground.
- **Alexanderplatz and Fernsehturm** — The large square and 368-meter TV Tower offer panoramic views (timed tickets recommended). The surrounding area mixes socialist-era architecture with modern shops.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Berlin eating is diverse, casual, and often excellent value.

- **Currywurst** — Sliced sausage with curry ketchup, typically served with fries. Classic spots include Curry 36 (Mehringdamm) and Konnopke’s Imbiss.
- **Döner kebab** — Ubiquitous street food; look for places with long local queues rather than pure tourist strips.
- **German classics and markets** — Try traditional plates in the Nikolaiviertel or at older restaurants such as Zur letzten Instanz. Weekend markets (e.g., in Prenzlauer Berg) are good for produce and snacks.
- **International influence** — Strong Turkish, Vietnamese, and Middle Eastern scenes reflect the city’s diversity. Prenzlauer Berg and Kreuzberg have strong café cultures.

Dinner service often starts around 18:00. Cards are widely accepted in central areas, though some smaller places prefer cash.

## Where to Stay

- **Mitte** — Most convenient for first visits; walking distance or short rides to Brandenburg Gate, Museum Island, and Reichstag. Higher prices and more tourists.
- **Kreuzberg** — Lively, diverse, with strong street-food and nightlife options (e.g., around Bergmannkiez or Kottbusser Tor). Good local feel.
- **Prenzlauer Berg** — Calmer residential area with cafés, parks, and 19th-century buildings. Popular with families and longer stays.
- **Friedrichshain** — Close to the East Side Gallery; energetic and nightlife-oriented.

The city is well-connected by transit, so most central neighborhoods work well.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and booking timing):

- **Budget:** €50–€80 — hostel or simple hotel, street food and market meals, day pass or walking, one paid museum.
- **Mid-range:** €120–€180 — mid-range hotel, mix of casual restaurants, transport, and two or three paid sights.
- **Comfort:** €200+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners, and optional experiences.

A BVG day ticket (Tageskarte, zones AB) is around €9–€11 and covers U-Bahn, S-Bahn, trams, and buses. The Berlin WelcomeCard adds museum discounts. Many outdoor sites and parks are free. City tax (€1–€7 per night) is often charged at check-in.

## Practical Tips

- Register online in advance for the Reichstag dome; popular museums also benefit from timed tickets.
- Public transport is efficient and honor-based (validate paper tickets). Inspectors issue €60 fines. The BVG app is useful for tickets and planning.
- English is widely spoken in tourist areas. Cards and contactless payment work almost everywhere in the center; carry some cash for smaller stalls.
- Best balance of weather and crowds is May and September. Summers (June–August) are busiest and warmest; winters are colder, darker, and cheaper. Shoulder seasons offer good value.
- Berlin is generally safe for visitors. Keep an eye on belongings in crowded transit hubs and tourist spots.
- The city is very bike-friendly; dedicated lanes are common. Walking is pleasant in the central districts.

## Conclusion

Berlin rewards visitors with a dense concentration of history, world-class museums, and distinctive neighborhoods. Prioritize the Brandenburg Gate and Reichstag area, Museum Island or the East Side Gallery, and time in Kreuzberg or Prenzlauer Berg. With reliable transit, clear booking systems for major sites, and a practical food scene, the city is straightforward to explore while remaining full of character.
