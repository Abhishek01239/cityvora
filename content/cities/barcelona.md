Barcelona is Catalonia’s capital on Spain’s Mediterranean coast, famous for Antoni Gaudí’s distinctive Modernisme architecture, a lively medieval core, excellent food and easy beach access. A three- to five-day visit covers the major landmarks while leaving time for neighbourhood walks, markets and meals.

## Places to Explore

- **Sagrada Família** — Gaudí’s unfinished basilica, begun in 1882 and still under construction, is the city’s defining landmark and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Book timed-entry tickets online well in advance (official site sagradafamilia.org); basic admission starts around €26 and includes an audio guide. The interior’s branching columns and stained glass are best experienced in person. Exterior views from the surrounding streets and Plaça de Gaudí are free.
- **Park Güell** — Mosaic-covered terraces, gingerbread-style gatehouses and panoramic city views. The Monumental Zone requires timed tickets (around €10); the outer park areas are free and offer good views. Reach via metro L3 to Lesseps or Vallcarca plus a short uphill walk, or take bus 24.
- **Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic)** — Medieval lanes, Barcelona Cathedral, Roman wall fragments and lively squares such as Plaça Reial. Visit early to avoid peak crowds. Free to wander.
- **Eixample and Modernisme buildings** — The 19th-century grid holds Casa Batlló, Casa Milà (La Pedrera) and the Block of Discord on Passeig de Gràcia. Many exteriors can be admired for free; interiors require tickets.
- **El Born / La Ribera** — Atmospheric streets near Santa Maria del Mar and the Museu Picasso. Good for walking and evening drinks.
- **Barceloneta and the beach** — The former fishing neighbourhood and long sandy beachfront. Popular for walks and seafood; the water is cleaner than in the past.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Barcelona offers strong value if you eat where locals do.

- **Menú del día** — Fixed-price weekday lunch (typically two or three courses plus drink and dessert) for roughly €12–€18 at neighbourhood restaurants. One of the best deals in the city.
- **Tapas and pintxos** — Small plates to share. Order several and share. Look for bars in El Born, Gràcia, Poble Sec (Carrer de Blai for inexpensive pintxos) or Sant Antoni rather than on La Rambla.
- **Local specialties** — Pa amb tomàquet (tomato bread), patatas bravas, fideuà (noodle paella-style dish) and fresh seafood. Avoid tourist-priced paella on La Rambla or the seafront.
- **Markets** — La Boqueria is famous but crowded and pricier at the entrance; Mercat de Santa Caterina or neighbourhood markets are better for eating.

Dinner is typically late (around 9 pm). Many kitchens close in the afternoon.

## Where to Stay

- **Eixample** — Excellent base for first visits: walkable to major sights, good hotel choice and calmer at night than the old town.
- **El Born** — Atmospheric, good restaurants and fewer crowds than the Gothic Quarter.
- **Gràcia** — More residential village feel with squares and local bars; convenient for Park Güell.
- **Gothic Quarter** — Central and atmospheric but noisier and more touristy at night.

**Hotel booking:** Search Barcelona hotels on major platforms; rates rise in summer and around major events. Book Gaudí sites early.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices vary by season and exact choices; 2026 figures):

- **Budget:** €60–€90 — hostel or simple hotel, bakery breakfast, menú del día lunch, casual tapas, metro, free sights plus one paid attraction every couple of days.
- **Mid-range:** €120–€180 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, daily paid attractions and local transport.
- **Comfort:** €200+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and optional experiences.

A T-Casual card (10 journeys on metro, bus and tram) costs around €11–€12. Single tickets are higher. Many parks, beaches and exterior views are free.

## Practical Tips

- Book Sagrada Família and Park Güell timed tickets online in advance; walk-up availability is limited or non-existent.
- Use the metro for longer distances. The T-Casual card is better value than single tickets. Citymapper or the TMB app helps with routing.
- Central areas are walkable; Park Güell and higher viewpoints involve hills.
- Pickpocketing is common on La Rambla, in the metro (especially Line 3) and around major tourist sites. Keep valuables in front pockets or a zipped bag and be aware in crowds.
- Best balance of weather and crowds is late spring and early autumn. Summers are hotter and busier; August sees some local closures.
- Tap water is safe. Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for small bars.

## Conclusion

Barcelona rewards visitors with its unique architecture, walkable neighbourhoods and strong food culture. Prioritise the Sagrada Família and a neighbourhood walk, then explore by interest. With reliable public transport and options at every budget, the city is straightforward to navigate while remaining rich in atmosphere and history.
