Idar is a compact North Gujarat town under the Aravalli hills, known for Idar Fort, Jain heritage and a walkable old market around the clock tower. It works well as a day trip from Himatnagar or an overnight stop on the way toward Polo Forest.

## Places to Explore

- **Idar Fort (Idargadh)** — hill fort with temples, ramparts and views over the town; start early and wear grip-friendly shoes.
- **Clock Tower** — landmark of the old market, useful as a meeting point for food and shopping walks.
- **Jain temples** — carved temples in and around the fort complex.
- **Idar hills** — short viewpoints above town; go in cooler hours.
- **Polo Forest** — forest and temple ruins about an hour away by road, often paired with Idar.

## Food & Local Food Areas

- **Clock Tower / old market:** Gujarati snacks, fafda-jalebi, gathiya and simple thali meals.
- **Bus stand area:** Practical breakfast and tea stops before a fort walk.
- **Station Road:** Local restaurants for Gujarati and North Indian meals.
- **Himatnagar (nearby):** Wider restaurant choice if you want a larger dinner stop.

Stall names and timings can change, so check the current vendor location locally.

## Where to Stay

- **Idar town:** Basic lodges and small hotels near the market and bus stand.
- **Himatnagar:** More hotel choice, about 30 km away, useful as a base for Idar and Polo.
- **Ahmedabad:** Possible day-trip start, roughly 2.5 to 3 hours by road.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding flights:

- **Budget:** ₹900–₹2,000
- **Mid-range:** ₹2,200–₹4,500
- **Comfort/premium:** ₹5,000+
- **Food:** roughly ₹250–₹1,000/day depending on street food vs restaurants.
- **Local transport:** roughly ₹100–₹600/day using shared jeeps, autos and cabs for the fort road.

Hotel prices vary by date, location and season; check live rates before booking.

## Two-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Clock tower market → Idar Fort and temples → evening snack walk.

**Day 2:** Polo Forest ruins → return via Himatnagar food stop.

## Before You Go

- October to March is generally more comfortable for the fort climb.
- Start the hill walk early in hotter months and carry water.
- Check temple timings before travelling.
- Keep comfortable footwear for uneven fort paths.
