Copenhagen is Denmark’s compact, design-conscious capital, known for its colorful Nyhavn waterfront, cycling culture, world-class restaurants and efficient public transport. A three- to four-day visit covers the historic core, major landmarks and neighbourhood walks without needing a car.

## Places to Explore

- **Nyhavn** — The iconic 17th-century canal lined with brightly painted townhouses and historic boats. Free to stroll; best in the early morning or at sunset for photos. Canal tours depart nearby and offer views of the harbour and Little Mermaid.
- **Tivoli Gardens** — Historic amusement park opened in 1843 in the city centre. Seasonal opening (including a Christmas season); buy tickets online. Combines rides, gardens, restaurants and evening lights.
- **Rosenborg Castle** — Renaissance castle housing the Danish crown jewels and royal collections. Timed tickets recommended. Surrounded by the King’s Garden, free to enter.
- **Amalienborg Palace** — The royal family’s winter residence with a popular changing-of-the-guard ceremony. Free to view the square; museum tickets for interiors.
- **Christiansborg Palace** — Houses the Danish parliament, royal reception rooms and royal stables. Various ticketed sections.
- **Other notes** — Strøget is Europe’s longest pedestrian shopping street. Christianshavn and the free-town of Christiania offer a different atmosphere. The Little Mermaid statue is a short walk or canal-tour stop from the centre.

The historic centre is flat and highly walkable or bikeable. Metro, buses and S-trains cover longer distances efficiently.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Danish food emphasises seasonal ingredients, rye bread and simple preparations, with a strong modern Nordic scene.

- **Smørrebrød** — Open-faced sandwiches on dense rye bread with toppings such as herring, shrimp, roast beef or egg. Classic lunch item; good versions at food halls like Torvehallerne.
- **Frikadeller** — Danish meatballs, often served with potatoes and gravy.
- **Bakeries and pastries** — Danish pastries (wienerbrød), cinnamon buns and excellent coffee. Many neighbourhood bakeries.
- **Food halls** — Torvehallerne is a popular covered market for sampling and casual meals.
- **Other** — Seafood is excellent; many restaurants focus on New Nordic cuisine. Tap water is excellent and free.

Dinner is typically earlier than in southern Europe. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; cash is rarely needed. Tipping is not expected; round up if pleased.

## Where to Stay

- **Indre By / Nyhavn area** — Most convenient for first-time visitors; walkable to major sights.
- **Vesterbro** — Trendy, good restaurants and nightlife, near Tivoli and the central station.
- **Nørrebro** — Diverse, local feel with cafés and parks; good metro links.
- **Christianshavn** — Canal-side, atmospheric, close to Christiania.

Book ahead in summer and around Christmas. The city is compact, so most central bases work well.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season):

- **Budget:** 500–800 DKK — hostel or simple hotel, bakery/food-hall meals, public transport or cycling, free sights plus one paid attraction.
- **Mid-range:** 1,000–1,600 DKK — mid-range hotel, mix of casual and restaurant meals, several attractions, transport.
- **Comfort:** 2,000+ DKK — nicer hotel, restaurant dining and experiences.

A 2-zone single ticket is around 24 DKK; a 24-hour City Pass Small (covering central zones including the airport) is around 100 DKK. Cycling is free if you rent a bike or use city bikes.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and daylight is May–September. Winters are cold and dark but atmospheric with Christmas markets and Tivoli lights. Summers have long days.
- Danish is the official language; English is widely spoken.
- Tap water is excellent. The city is very safe and bike-friendly; dedicated lanes are common.
- Public transport (metro, bus, S-train) uses zone tickets or contactless payment via app. The airport is well connected by metro.
- Many attractions have seasonal hours; check current opening times, especially for Tivoli.

## Conclusion

Copenhagen combines colourful historic waterfronts, royal landmarks, excellent design and a relaxed Nordic pace with outstanding food and easy cycling. Prioritise Nyhavn for the classic view, Tivoli for atmosphere, and leave time for smørrebrød and neighbourhood walks. With efficient public transport, strong cycling infrastructure and options across budgets, it is easy to explore while feeling both historic and contemporary.
