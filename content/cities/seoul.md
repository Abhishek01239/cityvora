Seoul is South Korea’s capital, a dense megacity where 600-year-old Joseon palaces sit beside neon districts, efficient subways and one of the world’s most dynamic food scenes. A three- to five-day visit covers the major historic sites, markets and neighbourhoods while leaving room for spontaneous cafe hops and evening walks along the Han River.

## Places to Explore

- **Gyeongbokgung Palace** — The largest and most iconic of Seoul’s five Joseon-era royal palaces, established in 1395. Wander the courtyards, throne halls and pavilions. The changing-of-the-guard ceremony typically occurs at 10 am and 2 pm. Free entry for visitors wearing hanbok (traditional dress); otherwise timed tickets recommended. Combine with the National Folk Museum on the grounds.
- **Bukchon Hanok Village** — A preserved neighbourhood of traditional wooden hanok houses between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung. Narrow lanes offer photo opportunities; visit between 10 am and 5 pm in designated zones to respect residents. Keep noise low.
- **Gwangjang Market** — Historic covered market known for street food. Try bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes), mayak gimbap and other snacks at long-standing stalls. Best in the late afternoon or evening.
- **N Seoul Tower / Namsan** — Take the cable car or hike for panoramic views of the city and Han River. Popular at sunset.
- **Insadong** — Traditional crafts, tea houses and galleries near the palace district.
- **Hongdae and Seongsu** — Hongdae for youthful energy, street performances and nightlife; Seongsu for industrial-chic cafes and design shops.

Other options include Changdeokgung (with the Secret Garden), the Han River parks for picnics, and day trips toward the DMZ.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Korean food is a highlight, from market snacks to tabletop barbecue and refined dining.

- **Street food and markets** — Gwangjang for classics; Myeongdong for egg bread and other snacks in the evening.
- **Korean BBQ and stews** — Shared grilled meats or dishes like samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup). Many restaurants use charcoal or table grills.
- **Cafes** — Seoul has an exceptional cafe culture; Seongsu and Hongdae offer stylish spots for coffee and desserts.
- **Convenience stores** — Excellent for quick, affordable meals (kimbap, ramen, snacks) available 24 hours.

Meals are often communal. Spice levels vary; ask for milder options if needed. No strong tipping culture.

## Where to Stay

- **Jongno / Myeongdong** — Central for historic sights and shopping; convenient for first visits.
- **Hongdae** — Lively, good for younger travellers and nightlife; well connected by subway.
- **Gangnam** — More modern and upscale; excellent transport links.
- **Insadong / Bukchon area** — Atmospheric base near palaces.

Book early for peak seasons. Apartments and hotels are widely available on major platforms.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices in KRW; confirm current rates as they fluctuate):

- **Budget:** ₩50,000–₩80,000 — hostel or simple hotel, market and convenience-store meals, T-money transport, free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** ₩100,000–₩180,000 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, paid attractions and experiences.
- **Comfort:** ₩200,000+ — nicer hotel, higher-end dining and optional tours.

A T-money card (about ₩3,000–₩5,000) is essential. Base subway fare is around ₩1,550 with free transfers within a time window. Load ₩20,000–₩30,000 to start.

## Practical Tips

- Get a T-money card on arrival (convenience stores or Incheon Airport) and top up with cash. Tap in and out on subways and buses for correct fares and transfers.
- The subway is efficient and English-signaged in central areas. Apps like Naver Map or Kakao Map are useful.
- Best times: spring (cherry blossoms) and autumn (foliage). Summers are hot and humid; winters cold but clear.
- Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for markets and smaller places. ATMs are common.
- Respect quiet hours and photo rules in residential hanok areas. Modest dress is not strictly required outside temples, but cover shoulders for some sites.

## Conclusion

Seoul blends ancient heritage with contemporary energy in a way few cities match. Prioritise Gyeongbokgung and Bukchon for history, Gwangjang for food, and a neighbourhood like Hongdae or Seongsu for the city’s modern pulse. With excellent public transport and options across budgets, it is easy to explore while discovering both iconic sights and everyday local life.
