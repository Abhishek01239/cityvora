Quito is Ecuador’s capital and one of the world’s highest, sitting at about 2,850 metres in a long Andean valley. Its Centro Histórico is a UNESCO World Heritage site with dense colonial churches, plazas and cobbled streets, while modern neighbourhoods stretch north. A focused three-day visit covers the historic core, viewpoints and local food while allowing time to adjust to the altitude.

## Places to Explore

- **Centro Histórico and Plaza Grande** — The colonial heart, with the presidential palace, cathedral and surrounding colonial buildings. Walk the grid of streets on weekday mornings for lighter crowds. Free and highly rewarding.
- **Iglesia de San Francisco and Plaza de San Francisco** — One of the largest religious complexes in South America, dating to the 16th century, with an impressive facade and interior art. The plaza is lively.
- **La Compañía de Jesús** — Ornate gold-leaf interior church. Timed or paid entry; book or arrive early.
- **Virgin of El Panecillo** — Large aluminium statue of the Virgin on the hill overlooking the centre. Take a taxi up for panoramic views of the valley and city; combine with sunset if timing works.
- **La Ronda** — Pedestrian street in the historic centre with restored colonial houses, bars and evening atmosphere. Popular for canelazo (warm cinnamon drink).
- **TelefériQo** — Cable car up the slopes of Volcán Pichincha for high-altitude views. Check weather and altitude readiness; not for the first day if sensitive.

Other options include Mitad del Mundo (equator monument, north of the city) and the modern north for parks and shopping. The historic centre is best on foot; use taxis or apps between neighbourhoods after dark.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Quito offers hearty Andean and coastal-influenced dishes that suit the cooler high-altitude climate.

- **Locro de papa** — Thick potato and cheese soup, often with avocado. A local classic, warming and inexpensive.
- **Hornado** — Slow-roasted pork served with llapingachos (potato patties) and pickled onions.
- **Empanadas de viento** — Puffy cheese-filled pastries dusted with sugar.
- **Markets** — Mercado Central for encebollado (fish stew), fritada and other stalls. Good for lunch and local atmosphere.
- **Other** — Canelazo in the evenings, fresh juices, and chocolate (Ecuador produces fine cacao). Many set-lunch menus (almuerzo) offer good value.

Meals are affordable. Cards are accepted in many places; cash is useful for markets and smaller spots.

## Where to Stay

- **Centro Histórico** — Most atmospheric for walking to churches and plazas. Stay in colonial buildings or hotels with courtyards. Use taxis or apps after dark.
- **La Mariscal / Plaza Foch** — Livelier with bars, restaurants and tour agencies. Convenient for nightlife and north-side access.
- **La Floresta or northern neighbourhoods** — Quieter residential areas with cafes and easier modern access.

Book ahead for peak dry season. Many hotels offer coca tea to help with altitude.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary):

- **Budget:** $25–$45 — hostel or simple hotel, market and set-lunch meals, local transport, free historic walking.
- **Mid-range:** $50–$90 — mid-range hotel, restaurant meals, a couple of paid sites or TelefériQo, taxis.
- **Comfort:** $120+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and guided experiences.

Taxis and ride apps are inexpensive for short trips. The new metro helps north-south travel. Walking covers the historic centre well.

## Practical Tips

- Quito sits at high altitude. Take the first day slowly: drink water, eat lightly, avoid heavy alcohol initially, and consider coca tea. Symptoms of altitude usually ease within 1–2 days.
- Weather can change quickly; sunny mornings often turn to afternoon rain. Pack layers, a light rain jacket and sun protection (strong even under clouds).
- Best drier months are generally June–September. Year-round visits work with proper clothing.
- English is spoken in tourist areas; Spanish is the main language. Cards work widely; carry some cash.
- Stick to well-lit, busy areas after dark in the historic centre. Use taxis or apps between neighbourhoods at night.
- Book popular sites or cable car if visiting in peak periods.

## Conclusion

Quito combines one of the best-preserved colonial centres in the Americas with dramatic Andean setting and accessible local food. Prioritize walking the historic plazas and churches, add the Panecillo viewpoint, and allow time for a market meal. With careful altitude pacing and reliable local transport, the city is straightforward to explore while remaining defined by its altitude, churches and valley light.
