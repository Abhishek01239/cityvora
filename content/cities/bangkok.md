Bangkok, Thailand’s capital (known in Thai as Krung Thep), is a dense, energetic megacity where ornate temples sit alongside elevated trains, busy markets and a world-famous street-food scene. The Chao Phraya River runs through the historic core, while the BTS Skytrain and MRT make much of the city navigable without a car. A focused three- to five-day visit covers the major landmarks, food areas and a couple of neighborhoods.

The city proper has roughly 5.5 million registered residents; the wider metro area is substantially larger.

## Places to Explore

- **Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha)** — The historic royal complex and Thailand’s most sacred temple. Entry is typically 500 THB; dress code requires covered shoulders and knees (scarves or trousers can often be rented at the entrance). Arrive early to avoid peak crowds and heat. Check official notices, as the site can close for royal ceremonies.
- **Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha)** — Home to the large reclining Buddha and a traditional Thai massage school. Entry around 300 THB. Compact and easy to combine with the Grand Palace area.
- **Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)** — Ornate riverside prang across the Chao Phraya. Best viewed from the river or at sunset. Entry around 200 THB. Cross by public ferry from the Bangkok side.
- **Chinatown (Yaowarat)** — Dense evening food streets, neon signs, gold shops and markets. Ideal for grazing rather than a formal meal. Walkable from the river or reachable by MRT.
- **Chatuchak Weekend Market** — Enormous weekend market (Saturday–Sunday) for crafts, clothing, plants and food. Take the BTS to Mo Chit or MRT to Chatuchak Park. Arrive earlier in the day for a less crowded experience.
- **Jim Thompson House** — Traditional Thai houses and a museum focused on the American businessman who revived the Thai silk industry. Paid entry; a calm contrast to the surrounding city.

Group the riverside temples together and use the Chao Phraya express boats for efficient, scenic transfers. Many outer areas are better reached by BTS or MRT.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Bangkok is one of the world’s great street-food cities. Stalls and small shops serve pad thai, boat noodles, grilled meats, mango sticky rice, som tam and countless regional Thai dishes.

- **Street stalls and markets** — Expect 40–80 THB for a basic plate in local areas. Chinatown (Yaowarat) is excellent in the evening; many neighborhoods have their own night markets.
- **Food courts in malls** — Clean, air-conditioned options with a wide range of Thai and international dishes at moderate prices.
- **Sit-down restaurants** — Local Thai places and international options are abundant; mid-range meals often run 100–250 THB.
- **Specialties** — Look for fresh fruit, Thai iced tea or coffee, and seasonal items. Many stalls are cash-only.

Tipping is not required but appreciated for good service in restaurants. Cards work in malls and many sit-down places; carry cash for stalls, markets and smaller shops.

## Where to Stay

- **Riverside / near Grand Palace (Rattanakosin, Thonburi side)** — Best for first-time temple visits and river atmosphere. Convenient boat access.
- **Siam / Chit Lom / Phaya Thai** — Central BTS interchange area with major malls, easy transit in multiple directions.
- **Sukhumvit** — Long commercial corridor with hotels, restaurants and nightlife; multiple BTS stations. Good mid-range and higher-end options.
- **Silom / Sathorn** — Business and dining district with solid hotel choice and river access via Saphan Taksin.
- **Khao San Road area** — Budget backpacker hub; lively but noisy. Useful for inexpensive stays and easy access to the old city.

Book ahead for the November–February high season and major holidays. Rates drop noticeably in the rainy months.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and exact choices):

- **Budget:** 1,000–1,800 THB — hostel or simple guesthouse, street-food meals, BTS/MRT or boats, free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** 2,500–5,000 THB — mid-range hotel, mix of stalls and restaurants, some Grab rides, one or two paid attractions.
- **Comfort:** 8,000+ THB — higher-end hotel, restaurant meals, more taxis or private transfers, premium experiences.

BTS fares are roughly 17–65 THB per ride; MRT is similar or slightly lower. Chao Phraya boats are inexpensive. Grab and metered taxis are widely available; insist on the meter for taxis. ATM withdrawal fees for foreign cards are common (often around 220 THB).

## Practical Tips

- Best overall weather is November–February (cooler and drier). March–May is hotter; May–October brings the rainy season with frequent short afternoon downpours. April includes the Songkran water festival, when streets can be wet and some businesses close.
- Dress modestly for temple visits (shoulders and knees covered). Light, breathable clothing is essential year-round; pack a light rain layer in the wet season.
- English is widely used in tourist areas, hotels and on transit signage. The Thai language is official.
- Transit is the most reliable way around: BTS Skytrain, MRT, Airport Rail Link from Suvarnabhumi, and river boats. Traffic is heavy; avoid relying solely on taxis during rush hours.
- Common scams include tuk-tuk drivers claiming temples are closed and offering “free” tours that end at shops. Verify information at official ticket offices.
- The city is generally safe for visitors who take normal urban precautions. Keep an eye on belongings in crowded markets.
- Visa-free entry rules for many nationalities were adjusted in 2026; confirm current requirements and complete any digital arrival forms in advance.

## Conclusion

Bangkok rewards a mix of classic sights and everyday urban energy. Prioritize the Grand Palace complex, Wat Pho and Wat Arun, then add Chinatown or Chatuchak and at least one river-boat ride. Strong public transit, outstanding and affordable food, and a wide range of accommodation make the city straightforward to explore at almost any budget.
