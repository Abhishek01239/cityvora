Halvad is a fortified old town in Morbi district, on the southern edge of the Little Rann of Kutch. It was a capital of the Jhala Rajputs: the new capital was established here in the late 15th century, traditionally dated to 1488 under Rana Rajdharji, before the court later centred on Dhrangadhra. The town sits by Samatsar lake. Local yellow sandstone and the pale soil give the streets a warm tone in low light. The 2011 census recorded about 32,000 people in the town. Halvad was in Surendranagar district until Morbi district was formed in 2013.

## Places to Explore

- **Samatsar lake** — the town tank. The west bank holds the dargah of Maula Qazi, an important Dawoodi Bohra pilgrim site. Walk the bank in the morning.
- **Historic mosque** — the old mosque in town shows Kathiawar building details. Treat it as a place of worship and follow local guidance on entry.
- **Stepwells and Shiva temples** — Halvad is said to have four old stepwells and a ring of Shiva temples. The Bhavani temple and Bhuteshwar Mahadev at the cremation ground are described as at least 500 years old and are protected monuments.
- **Old fortified core** — lanes of the former Jhala capital, best seen on foot rather than as a single palace ticket.
- **Little Rann edge** — Halvad is a practical southern approach toward the Wild Ass Sanctuary. Sanctuary entry, permits and vehicle rules are controlled; confirm the current gate before driving onto the rann.

## Food & Local Food Areas

- **Town market:** Kathiawadi vegetarian meals, bajra rotla, dal, kadhi and tea. Choice is local rather than a night-food street.
- **Highway dhabas:** on the roads toward Dhrangadhra, Morbi and the rann, simple Gujarati thali stops are the reliable option.
- **What not to expect:** a large restaurant scene. Carry water if you are heading onto the flats.

Stall names and timings can change, so check the current vendor location locally.

## Where to Stay

- **Halvad:** a few lodges and guesthouses. Confirm a room before arriving, especially in winter wildlife season.
- **Dhrangadhra:** a nearby town with more basic stays if Halvad is full.
- **Morbi or Dasada-side camps:** better if the trip is built around the Little Rann rather than the old town alone. Camp names and availability change by season.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding flights:

- **Budget:** ₹1,200–₹2,400
- **Mid-range:** ₹2,800–₹5,500
- **Comfort/premium:** ₹6,500+ if you add a rann camp
- **Food:** roughly ₹300–₹900/day.
- **Local transport:** town autos are cheap. A hired vehicle for Halvad plus a sanctuary approach is often ₹2,500–₹4,500 depending on distance and permit stops.

Hotel prices vary by date, location and season; check live rates before booking.

## Two-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Arrive from Morbi, Rajkot or Ahmedabad → Samatsar lake and dargah → mosque and old lanes → stepwell or temple walk if open.

**Day 2:** Early start toward the Little Rann only with a confirmed permit and vehicle → return via Dhrangadhra, or continue to Morbi.

## Before You Go

- October to March is the right season. Summer heat on the rann edge is severe.
- Wild ass sightings are not guaranteed, and driving on the flats without a local driver is a bad idea.
- Dress modestly at the dargah and temples. Ask before photographing people at shrines.
- Fuel and cash are easier in Morbi or Dhrangadhra than on the sanctuary tracks.
