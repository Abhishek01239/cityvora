Paris is France’s capital and one of the world’s most visited cities, known for its historic architecture, world-class museums, riverside boulevards and refined food culture. A three- to five-day visit covers the major landmarks while leaving time for neighborhood walks and meals.

## Places to Explore

- **Eiffel Tower** — The 1889 iron lattice tower remains the city’s defining landmark. It sparkles for several minutes every hour after sunset. Book timed entry tickets online in advance; views from the second level or summit are popular. The surrounding Champ de Mars offers free ground-level photos.
- **Notre-Dame Cathedral** — The Gothic cathedral on Île de la Cité reopened after restoration following the 2019 fire. Entry to the cathedral itself is free; the towers have a separate ticket. Combine with a walk across the nearby bridges.
- **Louvre Museum** — The world’s largest art museum, housed in a former royal palace with the glass pyramid entrance. Highlights include the Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo and Winged Victory. Book timed entry; the museum is closed on Tuesdays. A focused visit takes half a day.
- **Musée d’Orsay** — Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collection (Monet, Van Gogh, Renoir) in a former railway station. Closed Mondays. The large clock windows face the Seine.
- **Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur** — The hilltop basilica and artist square offer panoramic views. Visit early on a weekday to avoid peak crowds. Nearby lanes have cafés and street art.
- **Marais and Île Saint-Louis** — Historic district with narrow streets, independent shops, Jewish heritage sites and the Place des Vosges. Good for walking and people-watching.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Paris offers excellent food at every price point, from bakery breakfasts to classic bistros.

- **Bakery breakfast** — Croissant, pain au chocolat or baguette with café at a neighbourhood boulangerie. Often under €5.
- **Formule or menu du jour** — Fixed-price lunch menus at bistros (typically two or three courses) offer better value than dinner. Expect €15–€25 for a solid lunch.
- **Classic dishes** — Steak-frites, French onion soup, confit de canard, escargots and crêpes. Bouillons serve hearty traditional plates at accessible prices.
- **Markets and picnics** — Outdoor markets sell cheese, bread, fruit and prepared foods ideal for riverside or park picnics.
- **Wine bars and cafés** — Small plates and wine; many places expect a seated coffee rather than takeaway.

Always say “bonjour” when entering shops or restaurants. Service is usually included; tipping is optional.

## Where to Stay

- **Marais / Le Marais** — Central, walkable, historic atmosphere and good restaurants.
- **Latin Quarter** — Near the Seine, student energy, bookshops and proximity to Notre-Dame and the Left Bank.
- **Saint-Germain-des-Prés** — Elegant cafés and shopping; convenient for the Louvre and Orsay.
- **Montmartre** — More residential feel with views; quieter at night than central tourist zones.

**Hotel booking:** Search Paris hotels on major platforms; rates rise sharply in summer and around holidays.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices vary by season and exact choices):

- **Budget:** €70–€140 — hostel or simple hotel, bakery and market meals, metro singles, free sights plus one paid museum.
- **Mid-range:** €150–€250 — mid-range hotel, bistro lunches and dinners, museum tickets and local transport.
- **Comfort:** €300+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant meals and optional experiences such as a Seine cruise.

Metro single tickets are approximately €2.55 (loaded on a Navigo Easy card or phone). A day pass is available for heavier travel days. Many parks and the cathedral interior are free.

## Practical Tips

- Use the Métro for longer distances; the network is extensive and frequent. Load tickets on a Navigo Easy card or the official app. Paper tickets are no longer sold.
- Central neighbourhoods are walkable; many major sights are 20–40 minutes apart on foot.
- Book major attractions (Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Orsay) online in advance, especially in peak months.
- Best balance of weather and crowds is late spring and early autumn. Summers are warmer and busier; winters are cooler with lower hotel rates.
- Carry a reusable water bottle; tap water is safe. ATMs are widely available.

## Conclusion

Paris rewards both first-time visitors and return travelers with its layered history, exceptional food and compact layout of landmarks. Start with the classic sights along the Seine, then explore a neighbourhood by interest. With reliable public transport and options at every budget, the city is straightforward to navigate while remaining rich in atmosphere.
