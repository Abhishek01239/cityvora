Mexico City sits at high altitude on the site of the ancient Aztec capital Tenochtitlán. Its historic center centers on the vast Zócalo, one of the world’s largest public squares, surrounded by colonial architecture, while neighborhoods such as Roma Norte and Condesa offer tree-lined streets, parks and strong food scenes. The metro and Metrobús provide efficient transport across a large urban area.

## Places to Explore

- **Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución)** — The main square, free and open at all hours, with the Metropolitan Cathedral and National Palace on its edges. A giant Mexican flag is raised and lowered daily. Early morning or late afternoon avoids the heaviest crowds. Nearby is the Templo Mayor archaeological site.
- **Centro Histórico** — Compact historic core with colonial buildings, Diego Rivera murals (including in the National Palace), and Bellas Artes. Highly walkable for a day of sightseeing.
- **Roma Norte and Condesa** — Leafy, walkable neighborhoods with cafés, parks (Parque México), restaurants and a more residential feel. Popular bases for longer stays.
- **Other notes** — Chapultepec Park for green space and museums (Anthropology Museum is a highlight). Coyoacán for a quieter colonial atmosphere and Frida Kahlo connections. Day trips to Teotihuacan pyramids are common.

The historic center is flat and dense. Many museums close on Mondays.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Mexico City is a major food destination, with excellent street food and neighborhood markets.

- **Street tacos** — Al pastor, suadero and others from taquerías; often best after dark when the spit has been turning. Look for busy local spots a short walk from pure tourist areas.
- **Comida corrida** — Affordable set lunches (soup, main, drink) at fondas and neighborhood spots.
- **Markets** — Neighborhood markets for fresh juices, snacks and full meals at local prices. Mercado de Coyoacán and others offer variety.
- **Tips** — Eat where locals queue. Cards are accepted in many restaurants; cash is useful for street food and markets. Spice levels vary.

## Where to Stay

- **Roma Norte or Condesa** — Convenient, walkable, strong food and café scenes; good for first visits and evening returns on foot.
- **Centro Histórico** — Closest to the Zócalo and major sights; more budget options but check the specific block for evening comfort.
- **Polanco** — More upscale and polished.
- **Outer neighborhoods with metro links** — Often better value.

Book ahead for peak dry-season weekends and major holidays such as Día de Muertos.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and timing; roughly 18–20 MXN ≈ 1 USD):

- **Budget:** $40–70 USD (about 750–1,300 MXN) — simple room or hostel, street food and market meals, metro, free sights.
- **Mid-range:** $80–150 USD — mid-range hotel or apartment, mixed meals, some paid attractions and occasional rides.
- **Comfort:** $200+ USD — nicer hotel, restaurant dining and experiences.

Metro and Metrobús are inexpensive. Use ride-hailing apps rather than hailing street taxis. The city is generally safe in well-trafficked tourist and residential areas; standard urban caution applies.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of dry weather is March–May and October–November. June–September brings reliable afternoon showers; plan indoor options after lunch. Altitude is about 2,240 m — take it easy the first day and stay hydrated.
- English is spoken in many tourist and newer neighborhoods; Spanish is primary. Tap water is not recommended for drinking; stick to bottled or filtered.
- Many museums are free or low-cost on Sundays. Book popular sites or experiences ahead in peak periods.
- Wear comfortable shoes. Carry some cash for markets and smaller vendors.

## Conclusion

Mexico City offers a dense concentration of history on the Zócalo, excellent everyday food, and distinct neighborhoods within easy reach by metro or on foot. Prioritise the Centro Histórico for landmarks and a neighborhood such as Roma Norte for atmosphere and meals. With strong value, efficient public transport and layered culture, the city rewards both short visits and longer stays.
