Dubai stretches along the Persian Gulf as a modern city of record-breaking towers, man-made islands and a compact historic core along the Creek. The Burj Khalifa anchors Downtown, while the Marina and Palm Jumeirah offer waterfront living and resort beaches. The metro, taxis and ride-hailing connect the long coastal layout efficiently once you plan around distances.

## Places to Explore

- **Burj Khalifa and Downtown** — The world’s tallest building (828 m). Observation decks (At the Top on levels 124/125, higher options available) use timed tickets; book ahead, especially for sunset. Dubai Mall is attached, with free multi-hour parking on weekdays. The Dubai Fountain performs regularly on the lake (evening shows are free to watch from the promenade).
- **Dubai Marina and JBR** — A dense canal of towers with a long beachfront promenade (The Walk at JBR). Good for evening walks, restaurants and people-watching. Accessible by metro or tram.
- **Palm Jumeirah** — The palm-shaped artificial island with resorts, Atlantis Aquaventure waterpark and a monorail. Best as a half-day or dedicated visit; further from the centre.
- **Al Fahidi / Historic District and the Creek** — The older area with wind-tower houses, Al Shindagha Museum, textile and spice souks in nearby Deira. Abra (wooden boat) crossings on the Creek are cheap and atmospheric. A useful contrast to the glass towers.
- **Other notes** — Kite Beach or other public beaches for swimming. Museum of the Future has been closed for renovation in recent periods—check current status. Day trips to the desert or Abu Dhabi are common.

Many attractions use timed entry. The city is spread out; group nearby sights.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Dubai mixes Emirati, Levantine, Indian, Filipino and international options.

- **Emirati dishes** — Machboos (spiced rice with meat or fish), luqaimat (fried dough with date syrup). Look for them in older neighbourhoods or specialised restaurants.
- **Street and casual** — Shawarma, falafel, Indian thalis and mall food courts are widely available and affordable. Creek-side or Deira spots often have good local value.
- **High-end** — Rooftop and hotel restaurants with skyline views; book ahead for popular ones.
- **Tips** — Alcohol is available in licensed hotels and clubs. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory (10% is common in restaurants). Many places accept cards.

## Where to Stay

- **Downtown / Business Bay** — Closest to Burj Khalifa and Dubai Mall; convenient and well-connected by metro. Higher rates.
- **Dubai Marina / JBR** — Beach and nightlife atmosphere; good restaurants; further from historic areas.
- **Deira / Bur Dubai** — More budget-friendly, near the Creek, souks and airport. Practical for longer or value stays.
- **Palm Jumeirah** — Resort-style; best if the beach and luxury are the priority.

Book early for winter peak season and major events.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and timing):

- **Budget:** AED 250–450 (about $70–120) — simple hotel or hostel, casual meals, metro, free sights and one paid attraction.
- **Mid-range:** AED 600–1,000 — mid-range hotel, mixed meals, several paid sights and occasional taxis.
- **Comfort:** AED 1,500+ — nicer hotel or resort, restaurant dining, experiences and private transfers.

The metro is efficient and air-conditioned. Nol cards or contactless options work for transit. Taxis and ride-hailing (Careem, Uber) are plentiful but add up with traffic. The city is generally safe; standard caution applies.

## Practical Tips

- Coolest, most pleasant weather is November–March. Summers (June–August) regularly exceed 40°C with high humidity; many outdoor activities are limited. Rain is rare.
- English is widely spoken. Dress modestly in malls, souks and religious sites (shoulders and knees covered is respectful; beaches are more relaxed).
- Book popular tickets (Burj Khalifa, major parks) online in advance. Check opening status for sites under renovation.
- The metro Red Line links the airport to Downtown and Marina. Abra boats on the Creek are inexpensive and useful for short crossings.
- Tap water is treated but many visitors prefer bottled. Carry a light layer for strong air-conditioning indoors.

## Conclusion

Dubai offers dramatic modern architecture alongside a compact historic Creek district, excellent infrastructure and diverse food. Prioritise the Burj Khalifa and Downtown for the skyline, add the Marina or Palm for waterfront time, and spend a morning in Al Fahidi for contrast. With reliable metro links and options across budgets, the city is straightforward to explore in short or longer visits.
