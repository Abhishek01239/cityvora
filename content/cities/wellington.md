Wellington is New Zealand’s capital, a compact harbour city known for its wind, creative energy, excellent coffee and food, and easy access to nature. The centre is highly walkable, and a two- to three-day visit covers the main sights while leaving time for neighbourhoods and day trips.

## Places to Explore

- **Waterfront and Oriental Bay** — The harbour promenade is the city’s main outdoor space, with sculptures, cafés and views. Oriental Bay is a short walk for swimming or people-watching in summer.
- **Te Papa Tongarewa** — The national museum on the waterfront. Free general admission with strong Māori and natural-history collections. Allow at least two hours.
- **Cuba Street** — Pedestrianised street with cafés, independent shops, street art and a lively atmosphere. Good for wandering.
- **Wellington Cable Car** — Short ride from Lambton Quay up to the Botanic Garden and viewpoints. The ride and garden are popular with visitors.
- **Mount Victoria Lookout** — Short walk or drive for panoramic harbour and city views. Free.
- **Zealandia or nearby nature** — The ecosanctuary is a short bus or taxi ride for native wildlife. The city is also a base for day trips to the Wairarapa wine region or the Kapiti Coast.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Wellington has one of New Zealand’s strongest food and coffee scenes.

- **Coffee** — Excellent flat whites and specialty coffee are available throughout the centre. Many independent roasters.
- **Seafood and modern New Zealand** — Fresh fish, lamb and produce feature on menus. Look for places emphasising local ingredients.
- **Markets** — The weekend markets and waterfront food trucks offer casual options.
- **Bars and small plates** — The city has a good range of wine bars and casual dining, especially around Cuba Street and Courtenay Place.

Reservations are useful on weekends. Many places close earlier than in larger cities.

## Where to Stay

- **Waterfront / Lambton Quay** — Most central for museums and the harbour; convenient for first visits.
- **Cuba Street / Te Aro** — Lively with restaurants, bars and shops; good for evenings.
- **Thorndon** — Quieter residential area close to the centre and parliament.
- **Oriental Bay** — Waterfront views if available; slightly farther from the densest restaurant streets.

The city is compact, so most central bases work well. Book early for summer and major events.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary):

- **Budget:** NZ$80–NZ$120 — hostel or simple hotel, café meals, public transport, free museum and walks.
- **Mid-range:** NZ$150–NZ$220 — mid-range hotel, restaurant meals, cable car and local transport.
- **Comfort:** NZ$250+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and optional experiences.

Public transport (buses) is straightforward with a Snapper card or contactless payment. Many viewpoints, the waterfront and Te Papa’s general collection are free. Wellington is generally more affordable than Auckland for accommodation.

## Practical Tips

- The city is windy; a light jacket is useful year-round. Summers are mild; winters are cool and wetter.
- Best overall weather is December to February, though shoulder seasons have fewer crowds.
- English is the language. The city is very safe and walkable.
- Cards are accepted almost everywhere. Contactless payment is standard.
- Day trips to the Wairarapa wine region or Kapiti Island (wildlife) are popular.
- Ferries to the South Island depart from the harbour; useful if continuing a longer New Zealand trip.

## Conclusion

Wellington’s harbour, compact centre and strong food culture make it an easy and enjoyable city. Prioritise the waterfront and Te Papa, a walk along Cuba Street, and a viewpoint. With reliable transport and free outdoor spaces, it is straightforward to explore while remaining characterful and liveable.
