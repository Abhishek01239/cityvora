Rome, the Eternal City, layers more than 2,500 years of history into a walkable capital where ancient ruins sit beside Baroque fountains and modern life. A 3–4 day visit covers the major landmarks while leaving room to wander neighborhoods and enjoy the food.

## Places to Explore

- **Colosseum, Roman Forum and Palatine Hill** — The 1st-century amphitheater could hold tens of thousands for spectacles. Combined timed-entry tickets are required (book ahead via the official site, especially in peak season). The Forum and Palatine offer ruins of temples, arches and imperial residences; go early to avoid heat and crowds.
- **Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel** — Home to the Sistine Chapel ceiling by Michelangelo and extensive collections. Timed tickets essential; the walk through the museums can take 45+ minutes. Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) for St. Peter’s Basilica nearby.
- **St. Peter’s Basilica** — Free entry to the basilica itself (security lines can be long); dome climb is paid. Views over Rome from the top are excellent.
- **Pantheon** — The best-preserved ancient Roman building, with its massive unreinforced concrete dome. Now charges a small admission fee for most visitors; free on the first Sunday of the month.
- **Trevi Fountain** — Baroque masterpiece. Best early morning or evening to avoid peak crowds. Tossing a coin is a tradition for returning to Rome.
- **Piazza Navona and nearby** — Baroque square with Bernini’s Fountain of the Four Rivers. Easy walk from the Pantheon.

Other highlights: Galleria Borghese (advance booking required), Spanish Steps, and a stroll along the Tiber.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Roman cuisine is simple, ingredient-driven and excellent.

- **Pasta classics** — Carbonara (egg, pecorino, guanciale, black pepper—no cream), cacio e pepe, amatriciana and gricia. Best in trattorias in Testaccio, Trastevere or Monti.
- **Street and casual** — Supplì (fried rice balls with mozzarella), pizza al taglio (by the slice), porchetta sandwiches. Markets like Campo de’ Fiori for produce.
- **Gelato** — Look for places with metal containers (pozzetti) and natural colors rather than bright displays.
- **Coffee** — Stand at the bar for a quick espresso or cappuccino (cheaper than sitting). Breakfast is often cornetto and coffee.

Cover charge (coperto) of a few euros is normal. Tipping is not expected; rounding up is fine.

## Where to Stay

- **Centro Storico** — Around the Pantheon and Piazza Navona; walkable to most sights, atmospheric, higher prices.
- **Monti** — Charming streets, wine bars, near the Forum and Colosseum.
- **Trastevere** — Across the river; lively evenings, cobblestone streets, good food and nightlife.
- **Testaccio** — More local, excellent food scene, fewer tourists.
- **Near Termini** — Convenient for trains and metro; more varied quality, generally better value.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices fluctuate; confirm current rates):

- **Budget:** €70–€120 — hostel or simple hotel, street food and trattoria meals, public transport, one major paid site.
- **Mid-range:** €150–€250 — mid-range hotel, restaurant meals, multiple attractions, occasional taxis.
- **Comfort:** €300+ — nicer hotel, higher-end dining, guided experiences.

Public transport: single ticket €1.50 (valid 100 minutes on buses/trams, one metro entry). Day and multi-day passes available. Metro lines A and B cover most tourist areas. Walk a lot in the center.

## Practical Tips

- Best seasons: April–May and September–October for pleasant weather and manageable crowds. Summers are hot and crowded; winters milder with fewer visitors and lower prices.
- Book Colosseum and Vatican tickets in advance. Many sites have timed entry.
- Free drinking water from nasoni fountains throughout the center—carry a bottle.
- Modest dress required for churches. Be alert for pickpockets in crowded tourist areas.
- Cards widely accepted; some cash useful for small vendors. ATMs common.
- Avoid driving in the historic center (ZTL restricted zones).

## Conclusion

Rome rewards both planned sightseeing and unplanned wandering. Prioritize the Colosseum and Vatican with advance tickets, then lose yourself among the fountains, piazzas and side-street trattorias. The city’s layers of history make every walk feel like discovery.
