Hyderabad mixes Nizam-era heritage, busy Old City markets and a modern IT side of the city. Charminar, Golconda and Hyderabadi food make it an easy two- or three-day stop.

## Places to Explore

- **Charminar** — the city's iconic 16th-century monument and the heart of the Old City.
- **Laad Bazaar** — traditional market lanes beside Charminar, known for bangles and local shopping.
- **Chowmahalla Palace** — former Nizam palace complex with courtyards and ornate halls.
- **Golconda Fort** — historic fort with strong views and well-known acoustic features; allow half a day.
- **Salar Jung Museum** — large collection of art, antiques and artefacts; pick a few sections rather than trying to see everything.
- **Hussain Sagar** — central lake with the Buddha statue; useful for an evening walk.
- **Qutb Shahi Tombs** — historic tomb complex near Golconda for architecture lovers.

## Food & Local Food Areas

Hyderabad is famous for biryani, haleem, Irani chai and sweets.

- **Old City / Charminar area:** Classic stop for Hyderabadi biryani, haleem and kebabs. Look around Madina Circle and nearby lanes for long-running local places.
- **Paradise / Secunderabad:** Well-known for biryani and a convenient mid-city food option.
- **Irani cafés:** Try Osmania biscuits with Irani chai at traditional cafés near Charminar and older neighbourhoods.
- **Street snacks:** Lucchi, chaat, kebabs and local sweets are widely available; prices are usually modest.
- **Modern areas (Banjara Hills, Jubilee Hills, HITEC City):** Cafés, restaurants and a wider range of cuisines if you want a break from Old City food.

Stall names and timings change, so check current opening hours locally. Ramadan-season haleem is especially popular.

## Where to Stay

- **Old City / near Charminar:** Best for heritage sightseeing and food walks; can be busy and noisy.
- **Banjara Hills / Jubilee Hills:** Central, with restaurants, cafés and a range of hotels.
- **HITEC City / Madhapur:** Modern hotels, useful if you are combining travel with work or prefer a quieter base.
- **Near airport (Shamshabad):** Practical for short stays or early/late flights.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding flights:

- **Budget:** ₹1,500–₹3,000
- **Mid-range:** ₹3,500–₹7,000
- **Comfort/premium:** ₹8,000+
- **Food:** roughly ₹300–₹1,500/day (biryani and street food are affordable; hotel restaurants cost more).
- **Local transport:** roughly ₹150–₹600/day using Metro, buses, autos and cabs (Ola/Uber).

Hotel prices vary by neighbourhood, season and events. Check live rates before booking.

## Two-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Charminar → Laad Bazaar → Chowmahalla Palace → Old City biryani lunch → Salar Jung Museum → evening at Hussain Sagar.

**Day 2:** Golconda Fort (morning) → Qutb Shahi Tombs → return to the city for more food or a relaxed evening in Banjara Hills / Jubilee Hills.

With a third day, add a slower food crawl or a short outing beyond the main tourist core.

## Getting Around

Hyderabad has a Metro, buses, auto-rickshaws and app-based cabs. The Metro is useful for some corridors; autos and cabs are often easier for Old City and fort visits. Traffic can be heavy, so group nearby sights and allow extra travel time.

Rajiv Gandhi International Airport (HYD) is well connected; the airport metro and cabs link it to the city.

## Before You Go

- **October to February** is the most comfortable period for walking and outdoor sightseeing.
- April–June is very hot; start outdoor visits early and carry water.
- Dress modestly in the Old City and at religious sites.
- Bargaining is normal in markets; UPI is widely accepted.
- Check palace, fort and museum timings before you go — some close on specific days.

Hyderabad works well as a short city break focused on heritage and food, or as a base if you are already in South India for work or longer travel.
