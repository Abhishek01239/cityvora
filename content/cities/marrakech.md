Marrakech, one of Morocco’s imperial cities, sits at the northern edge of the High Atlas Mountains. Its red-walled medina (a UNESCO World Heritage site) centres on the busy Jemaa el-Fna square, while the modern Gueliz neighbourhood lies to the west. The city is known for its labyrinthine souks, historic palaces, traditional riads and as a gateway for Atlas and Sahara trips.

According to the 2024 Moroccan census, the Marrakech commune had roughly 1,002,697 residents. The wider prefecture is larger. Official languages are Arabic and Amazigh (Berber); Moroccan Arabic (Darija) and French are widely spoken, with English common in tourist areas.

## Places to Explore

- **Jemaa el-Fna** — The central square that functions as market by day and open-air performance space (storytellers, musicians, food stalls) by evening. Free to enter; arrive at dusk for the full atmosphere. Watch belongings in crowds.
- **Koutoubia Mosque** — The 12th-century Almohad minaret is the city’s tallest landmark and a model for later minarets. Non-Muslims cannot enter the mosque; the gardens and exterior views are open.
- **Bahia Palace** — 19th-century palace with carved cedar ceilings, tilework and courtyards. Adult entry around 100 MAD; open daily roughly 9:00–17:00. Arrive early to avoid queues.
- **Saadian Tombs** — Ornate 16th-century tombs rediscovered in the early 20th century. Entry around 100 MAD; small site that fills quickly — go at opening.
- **Jardin Majorelle** — Cobalt-blue garden and villa formerly owned by Yves Saint Laurent. Garden entry 170 MAD (book online in advance via the official site); timed entry recommended in peak seasons. The adjacent YSL Museum is separate.
- **Souks and medina lanes** — Covered markets selling spices, leather, carpets, metalwork and lamps. Bargain politely; many smaller workshops are free to browse.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Moroccan food in Marrakech ranges from street stalls to riad dining.

- **Tagine** — Slow-cooked stew of meat or vegetables with spices, preserved lemon or olives, served in the conical clay pot. Widely available from 40–80 MAD in local places.
- **Tanjia** — Marrakech specialty: meat with spices and preserved lemon sealed in a clay urn and slow-cooked in hammam ashes. Found in local restaurants and some markets.
- **Harira** — Tomato-and-lentil soup, often with chickpeas or meat. Common starter or light meal.
- **Street snacks** — Msemen (flaky flatbread), fresh orange juice on the square, grilled kebabs and snail soup (babbouche).
- **Pastilla** — Sweet-savory phyllo pie, traditionally with pigeon or chicken, almonds and cinnamon.

Jemaa el-Fna food stalls offer inexpensive evening meals. Many smaller medina restaurants prefer cash. Prices rise in tourist-facing places with rooftop views.

## Where to Stay

- **Medina riads** — Traditional houses with courtyards; atmospheric and walkable to souks and the square. Some are budget-friendly, others high-end.
- **Gueliz** — Newer neighbourhood with modern hotels, restaurants and easier taxi access.
- **Palmeraie** — Palm grove area with larger resorts and pools, further from the medina.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices vary by season; spring and autumn are busier):

- **Budget:** 300–500 MAD — hostel or simple riad, street/local meals, walking plus occasional petit taxi, free square and one paid sight.
- **Mid-range:** 800–1,500 MAD — comfortable riad, restaurant meals, several paid sights and local transport.
- **Comfort/premium:** 2,000+ MAD — higher-end riad or hotel, fine dining, private transfers or experiences.

Petit taxis (small local cabs) are inexpensive within the city; agree the fare or use the meter. The medina is best explored on foot.

## Two- or Three-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Morning at Bahia Palace and Saadian Tombs → medina souks → evening at Jemaa el-Fna for food and atmosphere.

**Day 2:** Jardin Majorelle (booked slot) → Gueliz or more medina time → optional hammam or rooftop sunset.

**Optional Day 3:** Day trip toward the Atlas foothills or Ait Benhaddou, or deeper medina wandering and a cooking class.

## Before You Go

- Best weather: March–May and September–November (mild days, cooler evenings). Summer regularly exceeds 35–40°C inland.
- Cash is essential in the medina and souks; cards work in many hotels and larger restaurants.
- Dress modestly when visiting religious sites or residential areas; comfortable walking shoes for cobblestones.
- Agree taxi fares or confirm the meter before starting. Be cautious of unsolicited “guides.”
- Book Majorelle Garden tickets online in advance during peak periods.
- The city recovered from the 2023 earthquake; major tourist areas are operational.
