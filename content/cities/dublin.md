Dublin is Ireland’s compact capital on the River Liffey, known for its literary heritage, Georgian architecture, lively pub culture and a mix of medieval and modern landmarks. A three- to four-day visit covers the main sights while leaving time for neighborhood walks, parks and traditional music sessions.

## Places to Explore

- **Trinity College and the Book of Kells** — Ireland’s oldest university, founded in 1592. The Old Library’s Long Room and the illuminated Book of Kells are major draws. Timed tickets are recommended; book online in advance, especially in peak season. Campus grounds are free to enter.
- **Temple Bar** — Cobblestone riverside quarter with pubs, live music and street atmosphere. Best visited in the evening for the scene; note that drink prices are higher here than in neighborhood pubs. Good for a stroll and photos.
- **Guinness Storehouse** — Ireland’s most-visited paid attraction at the historic St James’s Gate site. Self-guided experience through the history of the stout, ending with a complimentary pint at the Gravity Bar with city views. Book timed tickets online.
- **Phoenix Park** — One of Europe’s largest enclosed urban parks, home to wild fallow deer, the President’s residence (Áras an Uachtaráin) and open green space. Free entry; excellent for walking or cycling.
- **Georgian squares** — St Stephen’s Green and Merrion Square offer formal gardens and elegant terraces. Free and central for a relaxed walk. Nearby National Gallery and National Museum (Archaeology) are free.
- **Kilmainham Gaol and Dublin Castle** — Kilmainham Gaol is a powerful historical site linked to the 1916 Rising (book ahead). Dublin Castle grounds and parts of the complex are open for exploration.

Other options include the Ha’penny Bridge for Liffey views, Grafton Street for street performers, and free walking tours for context.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Dublin’s food scene ranges from traditional pub fare to modern Irish cooking.

- **Pub food** — Irish stew, fish and chips, and carvery lunches are common and good value outside tourist strips. A full Irish breakfast (rashers, sausages, pudding, eggs) is a classic start.
- **Guinness and pubs** — A pint is central to the culture. Neighborhood pubs away from Temple Bar offer better prices and atmosphere; many host free traditional music sessions in the evenings.
- **Markets and casual** — George’s Street Arcade and local delis for quick bites. Modern restaurants in areas like Portobello or the Docklands show contemporary Irish produce.
- **Sweets and snacks** — Soda bread, Tayto crisps and Murphy’s ice cream are local favorites.

Tipping is customary at around 10–12.5% in restaurants if service is not included.

## Where to Stay

- **South City / St Stephen’s Green / Grafton Street area** — Central for sights, shopping and restaurants; convenient first-visit base.
- **Temple Bar** — Atmospheric but can be noisy at night; better for short stays focused on nightlife.
- **North City / O’Connell Street area** — Often better value with good transport links; close to many hostels and mid-range hotels.
- **Ballsbridge or farther south** — Quieter residential feel with easy access by bus or tram.

Book early for summer and major events. Many options from hostels to boutique hotels.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season):

- **Budget:** €70–€110 — hostel or simple guesthouse, pub/café meals, Leap Card transport, free museums and parks plus one paid sight.
- **Mid-range:** €140–€220 — mid-range hotel, mix of casual and restaurant meals, several attractions, local transport.
- **Comfort:** €280+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and guided experiences.

A Leap Card is practical for buses, trams and rail (daily cap applies). Walking covers most of the centre. Book popular sites (Book of Kells, Guinness Storehouse, Kilmainham Gaol) online in advance.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and daylight is May to September. Shoulder seasons (April, October) have fewer crowds. Weather is changeable; carry a light waterproof layer.
- The centre is walkable and flat. Use Google Maps or the TFI app for routes. Avoid peak Temple Bar prices for drinks.
- English is the language. The city is generally safe; use normal urban caution with belongings in busy areas.
- Many major museums are free. Tap water is safe. Cards are widely accepted.
- Traditional music sessions are free in many pubs — arrive early for a seat.

## Conclusion

Dublin rewards a mix of landmark visits and unhurried time in its pubs, parks and Georgian streets. Prioritise Trinity College or the Guinness Storehouse, walk the Liffey and a square, then settle into a local pub for music and a pint. With compact distances, strong free culture and warm hospitality, the city is easy to enjoy while remaining deeply rooted in Irish history and daily life.
