Tokyo is Japan’s capital, a dense mix of ultramodern skyscrapers, historic temples, neon districts and world-class food. First-time visitors can cover the major highlights in three to five days using the efficient train and subway network.

## Places to Explore

- **Shibuya Crossing** — one of the world’s busiest pedestrian intersections; best experienced at dusk when the surrounding screens light up. Nearby Shibuya Sky offers an elevated view.
- **Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)** — Tokyo’s oldest temple, approached via the Nakamise shopping street of traditional snacks and souvenirs. Visit early morning to avoid crowds.
- **Meiji Shrine** — a wooded Shinto shrine in a forested park near Harajuku; a quiet contrast to the surrounding fashion streets.
- **Tokyo Tower and Zojo-ji Temple** — the red-and-white tower overlooks the 17th-century temple; a classic skyline photo spot.
- **Shinjuku Gyoen** — a large national garden with Japanese, French and English sections; popular for cherry blossoms in spring.
- **Tsukiji Outer Market** — still active for fresh seafood, street snacks and kitchen tools after the wholesale market moved.

## Food & Local Food Areas

Tokyo offers excellent food at every budget, from convenience-store meals to Michelin-starred counters.

- **Ramen** — look for Tokyo-style shoyu (soy) or shio (salt) broth with thin noodles; many shops use ticket machines. Expect ¥800–1,500.
- **Sushi** — conveyor-belt (kaiten) places for casual meals or counter omakase for higher-end experiences. Tsukiji Outer Market is convenient for daytime seafood.
- **Izakaya** — small pubs serving grilled skewers, small plates and drinks; Golden Gai and Omoide Yokocho in Shinjuku are atmospheric (book or arrive early).
- **Department-store food halls (depachika)** — high-quality prepared foods, bento and sweets in basements of stores in Shinjuku, Ginza or Tokyo Station.
- **Convenience stores (konbini)** — reliable, inexpensive meals and snacks available 24 hours; onigiri, sandwiches and hot foods are surprisingly good.

Stall and restaurant availability can change; check current hours locally or via maps apps.

## Where to Stay

- **Shinjuku** — excellent transport hub, many hotels, nightlife and restaurants; convenient base for first visits.
- **Shibuya / Harajuku** — youthful energy, shopping and proximity to major sights.
- **Asakusa** — quieter, closer to Senso-ji and traditional atmosphere; good for temple-focused stays.
- **Ginza / Tokyo Station area** — more upscale shopping and business hotels.

**Hotel booking:** Search Tokyo hotels on Booking.com or major platforms; prices vary widely by season and location.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (in JPY; roughly ¥150 ≈ $1 USD as a rough guide):

- **Budget:** ¥8,000–¥15,000 (hostels/capsule hotels, convenience-store and ramen meals, IC-card transport)
- **Mid-range:** ¥18,000–¥35,000 (business hotels, mix of casual restaurants and one nicer meal)
- **Comfort/premium:** ¥40,000+ (higher-end hotels, omakase or fine dining)

- **Food:** ¥1,500–¥5,000/day depending on style.
- **Local transport:** ¥500–¥1,500/day with Suica/Pasmo IC card; subway day passes available for visitors.

Hotel and attraction prices fluctuate; confirm live rates and hours before traveling.

## Practical Tips

- Get a Suica or Pasmo IC card (physical or mobile) on arrival; it works on trains, subways, buses and many shops.
- The JR Yamanote Line loop connects major stations including Shinjuku, Shibuya, Tokyo and Ueno.
- English signage is widespread in central areas; Google Maps is reliable for routing.
- Carry cash for smaller shops and temples; cards are widely accepted in larger places.
- Best weather for outdoor walking is spring (cherry blossoms) and autumn; summers are hot and humid, winters cool.

## Conclusion

Tokyo rewards both first-time visitors and return travelers with its layered history, food culture and efficient infrastructure. Start with the classic sights in Asakusa, Shibuya and Shinjuku, then explore neighborhoods by interest. With reliable public transport and options at every budget, the city is straightforward to navigate while remaining endlessly varied.
