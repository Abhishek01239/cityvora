Krakow is a compact historic city on the Vistula River, with a large medieval Main Market Square, a royal castle complex on Wawel Hill, and the atmospheric Kazimierz district. The core is highly walkable, and the city offers strong value in food, culture and architecture.

## Places to Explore

- **Main Market Square (Rynek Główny)** — One of Europe’s largest medieval squares, with the Renaissance Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) in the centre, St. Mary’s Basilica with its unequal towers and hourly hejnał trumpet call, and the Town Hall Tower. Free to wander; interiors paid. Underground museum beneath the square shows excavated medieval streets.
- **Wawel Royal Castle and Cathedral** — Hilltop complex with Renaissance palace, cathedral (burial place of Polish kings), and courtyards. Tickets for state rooms, treasury and cathedral interiors; grounds partly accessible. Allow half a day. Short walk from the Old Town.
- **Kazimierz** — Former Jewish quarter with synagogues (including Remuh and Old Synagogue), the Remuh Cemetery, Plac Nowy, and a lively mix of cafés, bars and street art. Strong atmosphere in the evenings.
- **Other notes** — Planty park ring around the Old Town; Vistula boulevards for walks; nearby day trips to Wieliczka Salt Mine or Auschwitz-Birkenau (book the latter in advance).

The historic centre is flat and pedestrian-friendly. Trams and buses cover the rest of the city efficiently.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Polish food is hearty and good value.

- **Pierogi** — Dumplings with fillings such as potato and cheese (ruskie), meat, or sauerkraut and mushroom. Widely available and affordable.
- **Żurek** — Sour rye soup with sausage and egg, sometimes served in a bread bowl.
- **Other plates** — Obwarzanek (ring-shaped bread from street carts), potato pancakes, and grilled meats. Milk bars (bar mleczny) offer simple cafeteria-style meals at low prices.
- **Tips** — Eat a few streets off the main square or in Kazimierz for better value. Cards accepted widely; some smaller spots prefer cash.

## Where to Stay

- **Old Town (Stare Miasto)** — Most central for first visits, walkable to the square and Wawel. Can be lively at night.
- **Kazimierz** — Excellent for atmosphere, food and bars; short walk or tram to the core.
- **Near Wawel / Stradom** — Convenient and slightly quieter.
- **Outer areas with tram links** — Better value for longer stays while remaining well connected.

Book ahead for spring, autumn and summer weekends. Many properties are in historic buildings.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and timing; €1 ≈ 4.2–4.3 PLN):

- **Budget:** 150–250 PLN (€35–€60) — hostel or simple room, pierogi or milk-bar meals, public transport, mostly free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** 300–500 PLN (€70–€120) — mid-range hotel or apartment, restaurant meals, castle or museum tickets, local transport.
- **Comfort:** 600+ PLN (€140+) — nicer hotel, guided experiences and nicer dinners.

A 24-hour public transport ticket is practical and inexpensive. Contactless cards work widely. The city is generally safe and easy to navigate.

## Practical Tips

- Best balance of weather and crowds is April–June and September–October. Summers are warmer and busier; winters are cold with Christmas markets on the square.
- English is widely spoken in tourist areas. Tap water is safe. Tipping around 10 % for good service is common if no service charge is added.
- Public transport (trams and buses) is efficient. Validate tickets or use the app.
- Book Auschwitz-Birkenau tours or entry well in advance if visiting. Wear comfortable shoes for cobbles.
- Pickpockets are rare but possible in crowded tourist spots; keep bags closed.

## Conclusion

Krakow delivers a dense concentration of medieval and Renaissance architecture, a lively Jewish quarter, and excellent everyday food within easy walking distances. Prioritise the Main Market Square, Wawel, and time in Kazimierz. With strong value and a compact core, the city is straightforward to enjoy while remaining richly historic.
