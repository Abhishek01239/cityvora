Hanoi, Vietnam’s capital, layers more than a thousand years of history into a compact, energetic city on the Red River. Once known as Thang Long (“Ascending Dragon”), it mixes ancient temples, French colonial boulevards, narrow tube houses and a vibrant street-food culture. A three- to four-day visit covers the core while leaving room for coffee stops and lakeside walks.

## Places to Explore

- **Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple** — The calm heart of the city. The scarlet Huc Bridge leads to Ngoc Son Temple on a small island. Early morning sees locals doing tai chi; evenings are lively with lights. Entry to the temple is modest (around 30,000 VND). Weekends often pedestrianize surrounding streets.
- **Old Quarter (36 Guild Streets)** — Dense lanes historically named for trades (Hang Bac for silver, Hang Gai for silk). Narrow tube houses, street vendors and constant motion. Ideal base for walking; everything of interest is within a couple of kilometres.
- **Temple of Literature (Van Mieu)** — Founded in 1070 as Vietnam’s first university. Courtyards, lotus ponds and stone stelae honouring scholars. Open daily; evening night tours on some days. A peaceful counterpoint to the Old Quarter.
- **Ba Dinh area** — Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum (check opening days and seasonal closures; usually mornings, closed Mondays and Fridays), One Pillar Pagoda and nearby historic sites. Group these into one morning.
- **French Quarter and Opera House** — Wider boulevards, colonial architecture and the Hanoi Opera House (modelled on the Palais Garnier). Calmer for evening strolls or performances.
- **Other highlights** — Imperial Citadel of Thang Long (UNESCO), Hoa Lo Prison Museum, and water-puppet theatre performances that continue a northern Vietnamese tradition.

Plan by district: the Old Quarter and lake are walkable; longer hops are better by Grab or cyclo.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Northern Vietnamese food emphasises clear broths, fresh herbs and balance rather than heavy sweetness.

- **Phở** — Breakfast classic. Northern style is lighter and more fragrant. Look for busy morning stalls; pho bo (beef) or pho ga (chicken).
- **Bún chả** — Grilled pork patties and slices with rice noodles, herbs and dipping broth. Traditionally a lunch dish; the stall where Obama and Bourdain ate remains a reference point.
- **Chả cá** — Turmeric-and-dill marinated fish fried tableside, served with peanuts, herbs and vermicelli. Cha Ca La Vong is the historic address.
- **Egg coffee (cà phê trứng)** — Invented in 1946 at Café Giang when milk was scarce. Rich, sweet foam over strong coffee; try it once as a treat.
- **Other street favourites** — Bánh cuốn (steamed rice rolls), bia hơi (fresh beer) and simple tascas-style meals. Eat where locals queue; cash is standard for stalls.

Prices are low: a bowl of pho often 40,000–70,000 VND. Cards work in larger places; carry small notes.

## Where to Stay

- **Old Quarter / around Hoan Kiem** — Maximum atmosphere and walkability. Expect noise and narrow stairs in older buildings.
- **French Quarter** — Wider streets, easier for first-timers wanting space and proximity to the Opera House.
- **West Lake area** — Quieter, more modern, good for longer stays or if you prefer fewer motorbikes outside the door.

Book ahead around Tet (Lunar New Year, late January/February) when much of the city slows and prices rise.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (confirm current rates; ~25,000 VND ≈ 1 USD):

- **Budget:** $25–45 — hostel or simple hotel, street food, walking + Grab, a few entry tickets.
- **Mid-range:** $60–120 — boutique hotel, mix of street and restaurant meals, comfortable transport, attractions.
- **Comfort:** $150+ — nicer hotel, restaurant dining and private transfers.

Grab (car or bike) is the reliable way to move. Airport bus 86 from Noi Bai is inexpensive. Most sights have modest entry fees.

## Practical Tips

- Best weather windows are October–November and March–April (milder, drier). Summers are hot, humid and rainy; winters can feel damp and cool (layers needed).
- Cross streets slowly and steadily; traffic flows around pedestrians. Use Grab rather than unmetered taxis from the airport.
- Cash remains essential for street food and small vendors. ATMs are common; exchange at reputable gold shops for better rates.
- English is useful in tourist areas but basic Vietnamese phrases help. Tap water is not recommended for drinking.
- Many museums and the Mausoleum close on certain days—check ahead. Comfortable shoes are essential for the Old Quarter’s uneven pavements.

## Conclusion

Hanoi rewards time spent on foot around Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter, punctuated by pho in the morning, egg coffee in the afternoon and a lakeside walk at dusk. Group the Ba Dinh historic sites into one outing, leave space for spontaneous street meals, and the city’s layered character—temples, colonial echoes and everyday energy—reveals itself without needing a packed checklist. With affordable food, reliable ride-hailing and a compact centre, it is an accessible yet richly textured introduction to northern Vietnam.
