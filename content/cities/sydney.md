Sydney sits on one of the world’s most recognisable harbours, with the white shells of the Opera House facing the steel arch of the Harbour Bridge. The city combines compact harbourside walking, beach suburbs, strong coffee culture and easy ferry access. A focused three- to four-day visit covers the icons, a coastal walk and a couple of distinct neighbourhoods without a car.

## Places to Explore

- **Sydney Opera House** — The UNESCO-listed building on Bennelong Point. Walking the exterior forecourt and steps is free. Guided tours of the interiors run daily (typically around 50 AUD for the standard one-hour tour; check current pricing and availability). Performances inside require separate tickets.
- **Sydney Harbour Bridge** — The free eastern pedestrian path offers classic views of the Opera House and Circular Quay. The Pylon Lookout is a low-cost option; the full BridgeClimb is a paid experience booked in advance.
- **Circular Quay and The Rocks** — Ferry hub, sandstone lanes and colonial-era buildings directly beside the two icons. Weekend markets in The Rocks are popular.
- **Royal Botanic Garden** — Free harbourside green space next to the Opera House, good for a short walk or picnic.
- **Bondi to Coogee Coastal Walk** — Roughly 6 km clifftop path linking Bondi, Tamarama, Bronte, Clovelly and Coogee. Most people allow 2–3 hours of walking plus beach stops. Buses run from either end.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Sydney’s dining draws on its coastline and multicultural population. Seafood is a reliable highlight; coffee and brunch culture is especially strong in Surry Hills and the Inner West.

- **Surry Hills** — Dense cluster of cafés, bakeries and restaurants. Classic breakfast spots and independent coffee roasters sit among Victorian terrace streets a short distance from the CBD.
- **Harbour and Fish Market areas** — Grilled fish, oysters and seafood plates near Darling Harbour or the newer market precinct.
- **Everyday options** — Café breakfasts, pub meals and casual Asian or Mediterranean places. Many neighbourhoods support good mid-range dining without reservation pressure on weekdays.

Tipping is not mandatory; rounding up or a small gratuity for good service is common.

## Where to Stay

- **Circular Quay / The Rocks** — Walking distance to the Opera House and Bridge; higher rates but saves transport time.
- **Surry Hills** — Strong food and café scene, short bus or walk to the harbour.
- **Bondi** — Beach atmosphere; adds commute time to the city centre.
- **Sydney Central / Darling Harbour** — Practical for arrivals and mixed sightseeing.

Business hotels and apartments offer solid mid-range value outside peak summer and major events.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices rise in December–February and around major events):

- **Budget:** AUD 100–160 — hostel or budget room, café/market meals, public transport, free harbour walks and beaches.
- **Mid-range:** AUD 200–350 — comfortable hotel, mixed restaurant meals, Opera House tour or paid sights, ferries and buses.
- **Comfort/premium:** AUD 400+ — harbour-view hotel, seafood dinners, taxis or private experiences.

Opal card or contactless payments cover buses, trains and ferries. A ferry ride on the harbour is both transport and sightseeing.

## Three-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Circular Quay → Opera House exterior and optional tour → Botanic Garden → Harbour Bridge pedestrian path or The Rocks.

**Day 2:** Bondi Beach morning → Bondi to Coogee Coastal Walk (or reverse) with beach stops → return by bus.

**Day 3:** Surry Hills cafés and lunch → optional ferry to Manly or a short visit to another inner suburb (Newtown or Paddington) → evening harbour views.

## Before You Go

- Best weather and fewer extreme crowds: September–November and March–May. Summer (December–February) is warmer, busier and more expensive, especially around beaches and New Year’s Eve.
- The city is very safe and walkable in the harbour core; use public transport or ferries for beaches and outer suburbs.
- Book Opera House tours and any BridgeClimb sessions ahead in peak periods.
- English is the main language. Tap water is safe.
- Check current ferry and bus routes; the Opal system is straightforward for visitors.
