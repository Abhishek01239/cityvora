Cape Town sits where Table Mountain meets the Atlantic, combining dramatic natural scenery, layered history and a vibrant food culture. A four- to seven-day visit covers the major landmarks while leaving time for neighbourhood walks, markets and a day on the peninsula.

## Places to Explore

- **Table Mountain** — The flat-topped landmark that defines the city. Take the rotating Aerial Cableway for a quick ascent or hike routes such as Platteklip Gorge. Views on clear days stretch across Table Bay toward Robben Island. The cableway closes in high winds; check conditions and book timed tickets online in advance, especially in peak months.
- **V&A Waterfront** — Working harbour with restaurants, shops, the Two Oceans Aquarium and the Nelson Mandela Gateway. Catch the ferry to Robben Island from here. It is busy but convenient and generally safe for evening walks.
- **Robben Island** — Former political prison where Nelson Mandela was held. Tours are led by former inmates and include the prison block. Ferries are weather-dependent and sell out in high season; book weeks ahead.
- **Bo-Kaap** — Colourful historic neighbourhood with Cape Malay heritage, steep streets and the Bo-Kaap Museum. Respect residents; it is a living community.
- **Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden** — On the eastern slopes of Table Mountain, featuring indigenous fynbos and forest trails. Good for a quieter half-day.
- **Boulders Beach** — African penguin colony in the southern peninsula. Combine with a Cape Point day trip via Chapman’s Peak Drive.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Cape Town offers excellent value across budgets, from market stalls to contemporary restaurants.

- **Local specialities** — Bobotie (spiced minced meat bake), Cape Malay curry, Gatsby (oversized sandwich), boerewors and braai (barbecue). Fresh fish and chips are widely available near harbours such as Kalk Bay and Hout Bay.
- **Markets** — Oranjezicht City Farm Market, V&A markets and neighbourhood food halls serve everything from oysters and pizza to traditional dishes.
- **Everyday meals** — Bakery breakfasts, street food and mid-range restaurants in areas such as Kloof Street or Sea Point keep daily food costs moderate.
- **Wine** — Day trips to Stellenbosch or Constantia offer tastings; the city itself has many wine bars.

Most places accept cards; carry small cash for tips and parking attendants. Service is often included; 10% is common if not.

## Where to Stay

- **City Bowl / Gardens** — Walkable, close to the mountain and central sights.
- **Sea Point / Green Point** — Beachfront promenade, restaurants and good transport links.
- **Bo-Kaap / Tamboerskloof** — Historic character and proximity to the city centre.
- **False Bay (Muizenberg, Kalk Bay)** — More relaxed coastal feel, warmer water and cafés.

Book ahead for December–January and major events; rates rise sharply then.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices vary by season; confirm current exchange rates):

- **Budget:** R600–R1,000 — hostel or simple guesthouse, market and casual meals, public transport or occasional Uber, free or low-cost sights.
- **Mid-range:** R1,500–R2,500 — mid-range hotel or apartment, restaurant meals, one paid attraction or tour, local transport.
- **Comfort:** R4,000+ — higher-end hotel, fine dining and private transfers or experiences.

Uber is widely used for short trips. MyCiTi buses serve some routes. A day on the Cape Peninsula or winelands adds car-rental or tour costs.

## Practical Tips

- Book Table Mountain Cableway and Robben Island ferry tickets online in advance. Both are weather-dependent.
- Best balance of weather and crowds is March–May and September–November. Summers (Nov–Feb) are warm but busier; winters are milder but wetter.
- Tap water is generally safe. Carry a reusable bottle.
- Daytime in tourist areas is generally fine; use Uber rather than walking unfamiliar streets after dark and keep valuables secure.
- English is widely spoken. The local currency is the South African Rand (ZAR).

## Conclusion

Cape Town combines iconic natural scenery with rich history and accessible food and neighbourhoods. Prioritise Table Mountain and Robben Island early in your stay, then explore the waterfront, Bo-Kaap and the peninsula according to weather and interest. With reliable ride-hailing, varied accommodation and options at every budget, the city is straightforward to navigate while offering lasting atmosphere.
