Athens is the capital of Greece and one of the world’s oldest continuously inhabited cities, layered with classical monuments, Byzantine churches, 19th-century neoclassical streets and a lively modern food scene. The historic centre is compact and walkable, with most major ancient sites clustered around the Acropolis hill.

A focused three- to five-day visit covers the Acropolis and museum, the old neighbourhoods, and at least one evening meal in a local taverna.

## Places to Explore

- **Acropolis and Parthenon** — The ancient citadel rising above the city, dominated by the 5th-century BC Parthenon. Timed-entry tickets are essential in peak season (book via the official site). Allow 2–3 hours. Free on the first and third Sundays from November to March. Views from the Propylaea and the surrounding walls are excellent.
- **Acropolis Museum** — Modern museum at the foot of the hill with sculptures, architectural fragments and the Parthenon frieze. Book timed entry. The glass floors reveal excavated ruins below. Combine easily with the Acropolis on the same morning.
- **Plaka and Anafiotika** — The oldest residential quarter on the slopes of the Acropolis. Narrow lanes, neoclassical houses, Byzantine churches and souvenir shops. Anafiotika is a small cluster of whitewashed island-style houses higher up. Early morning or late afternoon is quieter.
- **Ancient Agora and Roman Agora** — The commercial and civic heart of classical Athens, with the well-preserved Temple of Hephaestus. Nearby Roman Agora and Hadrian’s Library are compact. Each site requires its own ticket.
- **Monastiraki and Psyrri** — Monastiraki Square with the metro station, flea market lanes and views toward the Acropolis. Psyrri to the north has tavernas, street art and evening energy.
- **Filopappou Hill or Lycabettus Hill** — Free viewpoints. Filopappou offers classic Acropolis perspectives; Lycabettus has a funicular or a steep walk for city-wide panoramas.

The core ancient sites and Plaka are best explored on foot. Group them by area to minimize walking in the heat.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Athens offers excellent value for Greek food, from street souvlaki to sit-down meze in tavernas.

- **Souvlaki and gyros** — Grilled pork or chicken skewers, or vertical-spit meat, wrapped in pita with tomato, onion and tzatziki. Widely available for €3–5 from counters; a full plate is higher.
- **Tavernas** — Shared small plates (meze) such as Greek salad, gigantes (baked beans), grilled halloumi, and grilled meats. House wine by the carafe is common. Psyrri, Koukaki and areas away from the busiest Plaka streets tend to be better value.
- **Central Market (Varvakios)** — On Athinas Street for meat, fish, spices, cheese and olives. Good for a morning look and casual eats nearby.
- **Sweet stops** — Baklava, loukoumades or a Greek coffee in a neighbourhood café.

Tipping is not obligatory; rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service is appreciated. Cards are widely accepted; keep some cash for small places.

## Where to Stay

- **Plaka / Monastiraki** — Most atmospheric and closest to the Acropolis for first visits. Higher rates and more foot traffic.
- **Koukaki / Makrygianni** — Residential streets south of the Acropolis Museum. Quieter, walkable to sights, good café scene.
- **Syntagma** — Practical base near the metro hub and shopping streets.
- **Kolonaki** — More polished, with boutiques and cafés; short walk or metro to the centre.
- **Psyrri / Gazi** — Livelier evenings and dining.

Book ahead for April–May, September–October and major holidays. Rates drop noticeably in winter.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and exact choices):

- **Budget:** €45–€70 — hostel or simple hotel, souvlaki and taverna meals, metro day pass, free hills and walks, one paid site.
- **Mid-range:** €80–€140 — mid-range hotel, mix of restaurant meals, Acropolis + museum and local transport.
- **Comfort:** €160+ — higher-end hotel, restaurant dinners and premium experiences.

Metro/bus/tram 90-minute ticket is €1.20; 24-hour pass €4.10 (city only). Airport metro is €9 one way. Contactless bank cards work with a daily cap on city travel.

## Practical Tips

- Best overall weather is April–May and September–October. July–August is hot (often above 30°C) and crowded; the Acropolis may close midday in extreme heat. November–March is milder, quieter and cheaper, with free entry on certain Sundays.
- Pack layers, comfortable walking shoes and sun protection. Evenings can cool down.
- English is widely spoken in tourist areas. Tap water is generally safe. Cards work almost everywhere; carry a little cash.
- Metro Lines 1–3 cover the centre efficiently. Line 3 reaches the airport. Walking is ideal in Plaka and around the Acropolis.
- The city is generally safe for visitors (US Level 1 advisory). Watch for pickpockets in crowded tourist spots and on public transport. Stick to well-lit main streets at night.
- Book Acropolis and popular museum tickets online in advance during busy periods.

## Conclusion

Athens rewards a mix of ancient monuments and neighbourhood wandering. Prioritize the Acropolis and its museum, then spend time in Plaka and Monastiraki before choosing a viewpoint or a longer taverna meal. With strong walkability, affordable food and efficient metro, the city is straightforward to explore while remaining defined by its layered history and lively street life.
