Budapest is two cities joined by the Danube: hilly Buda on the west bank and flatter Pest on the east. The illuminated Parliament, Chain Bridge and Castle Hill define the skyline, while thermal baths and a lively food and bar scene fill the days and evenings. A three- to four-day visit covers the major sights without constant rushing.

## Places to Explore

- **Hungarian Parliament** — Neo-Gothic building on the Pest riverfront. Interior tours are timed and should be booked online in advance (around 14,000 HUF for non-EEA visitors, lower for EEA). The exterior is free to admire day or night.
- **Chain Bridge and river promenade** — The oldest permanent bridge across the Danube. Free to cross; especially atmospheric after dark when the Parliament is lit. The Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial is nearby.
- **Castle Hill (Buda)** — Buda Castle complex, Matthias Church with its colourful roof, and Fisherman’s Bastion (upper terraces paid during the day, lower areas free). Reach by bus, funicular or a steep walk. Allow half a day for views and wandering.
- **St. Stephen’s Basilica** — Large Pest basilica with a paid dome climb for city views. Easy to combine with a Parliament exterior walk.
- **Thermal baths** — Széchenyi in City Park is the classic first choice (outdoor pools, around 13,200 HUF weekdays with locker). Rudas and others offer different atmospheres. Gellért has been closed for renovation. Arrive early for shorter queues.
- **Jewish Quarter and ruin bars** — Dohány Street Synagogue (closed Saturdays) and the surrounding streets with ruin bars that open in the evening. Great Market Hall is closed Sundays.

Most central sights are walkable or a short tram/metro ride apart. The city is easy to navigate once the two sides of the river are clear.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Hungarian cooking is paprika-driven, hearty and often excellent value outside the most touristy riverfront spots.

- **Goulash (gulyás)** — Traditionally a soup with beef, paprika, potatoes and carrots. What many expect as a thick stew is closer to pörkölt.
- **Other local plates** — Chicken paprikash with nokedli (small dumplings), lángos (fried dough with toppings), and chimney cake (kürtőskalács).
- **Markets** — Great Market Hall for produce, paprika and casual meals. Neighbourhood spots for better value.
- **Ruin bars** — Former abandoned buildings turned into multi-room bars in the Jewish Quarter; order food or drinks and explore the spaces.

Lunch specials (napi menü) offer good value. Reservations help at popular restaurants in the evening.

## Where to Stay

- **Pest centre (near Deák or Kálvin)** — Most convenient for first visits, restaurants and transport. Can be lively at night.
- **Castle Hill / Tabán (Buda)** — Quieter, scenic, closer to the castle views. Steeper and slightly less central for evening options.
- **Jewish Quarter** — Excellent for food and bars; can be noisy on weekends.
- **Near City Park or outer Pest** — More residential, good for baths access and longer stays, with solid metro links.

Book ahead for spring, autumn and summer weekends. Many properties are in older buildings with character.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and booking timing; €1 ≈ 360–370 HUF):

- **Budget:** 12,000–20,000 HUF (€35–€55) — hostel or simple room, market or lunch-menu meals, public transport, mostly free sights plus one bath.
- **Mid-range:** 25,000–45,000 HUF (€70–€125) — mid-range hotel or apartment, restaurant meals, Parliament or bath ticket, local transport.
- **Comfort:** 55,000+ HUF (€150+) — nicer hotel, guided experiences and nicer dinners.

A 72-hour transport pass is practical. Contactless cards are widely accepted; pay in forints. The Budapest Card can be worthwhile if you plan multiple paid sites and transport in a short stay.

## Practical Tips

- Book Parliament tours online in advance. Thermal baths are best early on weekdays.
- Castle Hill streets and many river views are free. Fisherman’s Bastion upper levels have a daytime fee.
- Best weather and crowd balance is April–June and September–October. July–August is hotter and busier; winters are cold and atmospheric with Christmas markets.
- English is widely spoken in tourist areas. Tap water is safe. Tipping 10 % in restaurants is common if service is good.
- Public transport (metro, trams, buses) is efficient. Validate tickets or use the app.
- Pickpockets operate in crowded tourist areas and on trams; keep bags closed and in front.

## Conclusion

Budapest delivers strong visual drama from the river and Castle Hill, combined with the practical pleasure of thermal baths and solid local food. Prioritise the Parliament exterior (and tour if available), a crossing of Chain Bridge, time on Castle Hill, and at least one bath session. With careful ticket planning and a relaxed pace, the city is easy to enjoy while remaining richly layered.
