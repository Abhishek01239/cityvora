Kyoto served as Japan’s imperial capital for more than a thousand years and retains a dense concentration of temples, shrines, traditional wooden townhouses (machiya) and refined seasonal cuisine. Seventeen UNESCO World Heritage sites sit within or near the city. A focused two- or three-day visit covers the best-known landmarks and food areas without requiring a car.

## Places to Explore

- **Fushimi Inari Taisha** — Thousands of vermilion torii gates form a tunnel up Mount Inari. Free and open 24 hours; arrive early or late to reduce crowds. Allow 1–2 hours for the lower paths or half a day for the full climb.
- **Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)** — Gold-leafed Zen temple reflected in a pond. Entry around ¥500; open roughly 9:00–17:00. Compact grounds make it an easy half-day stop with nearby gardens.
- **Kiyomizu-dera** — Wooden stage temple on a hillside with city views. Entry around ¥400; best in clear weather. Combine with a walk through the nearby Higashiyama lanes.
- **Arashiyama Bamboo Grove** — Dense bamboo path on the western edge of the city. Free; pair with the nearby monkey park or riverside walks. Mornings are quieter.
- **Gion and Higashiyama** — Historic geisha district and preserved wooden streets. Evening walks reveal lantern-lit atmosphere; respect private alley photography rules.
- **Nishiki Market** — Covered “Kyoto’s Kitchen” market for local ingredients, pickles, sweets and street snacks. Best on weekday mornings.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Kyoto’s cooking developed around the imperial court and Zen temples: seasonal, lightly seasoned and vegetable-forward.

- **Kaiseki** — Multi-course seasonal cuisine, often ¥8,000–¥20,000+ for a set. Available in Gion and temple-adjacent restaurants; book ahead for formal versions.
- **Yudofu** — Simmered tofu in kombu broth, a classic temple-influenced dish. Found in Arashiyama and near major temples, typically ¥2,500–¥6,000.
- **Nishiki Market snacks** — Pickles, tofu products, matcha sweets, grilled items and fresh produce. Easy and affordable tasting walk.
- **Matcha and wagashi** — Uji tea and traditional sweets. Tea-ceremony experiences start around ¥2,500.
- Everyday options include udon, soba, ramen and convenience-store meals (¥500–¥1,500).

Many smaller temples and traditional restaurants remain cash-only. Carry yen.

## Where to Stay

- **Gion / Higashiyama** — Walking distance to temples and evening atmosphere; higher rates in peak seasons.
- **Kyoto Station area** — Excellent train and subway connections, more modern hotels, convenient for day trips.
- **Arashiyama** — Quieter riverside setting if the bamboo grove and western temples are the priority.
- Business hotels and guesthouses city-wide offer mid-range value outside cherry-blossom and autumn-foliage peaks.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (prices vary by season; peak March–April and November are higher):

- **Budget:** ¥6,000–¥10,000 — hostel or guesthouse, market/convenience food, bus day pass, free temples.
- **Mid-range:** ¥15,000–¥25,000 — business hotel, mixed restaurant meals, paid temples and local transport.
- **Comfort/premium:** ¥30,000+ — higher-end hotel or ryokan, kaiseki dinner, taxis or private experiences.

City bus flat fare is around ¥230; a Subway & Bus day pass is available. Many top sights (Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama bamboo path) are free.

## Two-Day Plan

**Day 1:** Fushimi Inari (early) → Nishiki Market lunch and snacks → Higashiyama / Kiyomizu-dera → Gion evening walk.

**Day 2:** Kinkaku-ji → Arashiyama Bamboo Grove and riverside → optional tea or yudofu meal → return via Kyoto Station area if needed.

## Before You Go

- Best weather and scenery: late March–early April (sakura) and mid-November (autumn foliage); book lodging well ahead.
- Shoulder seasons (May–June, September–October) offer fewer crowds and better rates.
- Kyoto is compact enough for bus + walking; the city is very safe.
- Check current temple hours and any temporary restrictions before visiting.
- Carry cash for smaller temples, markets and traditional eateries.
