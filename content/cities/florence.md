Florence (Firenze), the cradle of the Italian Renaissance, sits on the Arno River with a compact historic centre that is almost entirely walkable. The massive dome of the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore dominates the skyline, while narrow streets lead to world-class museums, artisan workshops and trattorias. A three- to four-day visit covers the major sights without constant rushing.

## Places to Explore

- **Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore (Duomo)** — The cathedral itself is free to enter (closed Sundays and during services). Climbing Brunelleschi’s dome requires the Brunelleschi Pass (€30 adult), which includes the dome, Giotto’s bell tower, Baptistery, Opera del Duomo Museum and Santa Reparata crypt; book a timed dome slot well ahead in peak season.
- **Uffizi Gallery** — One of the world’s great collections of Renaissance painting (Botticelli, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael). Adult tickets are €25 on the day or €29 booked in advance; from 16:00 the price drops to €16 (€20 booked). Closed Mondays. Timed entry is essential.
- **Galleria dell’Accademia** — Home to Michelangelo’s David. Adult ticket €20 plus a €4 reservation fee when booked online. Closed Mondays.
- **Ponte Vecchio** — Medieval stone bridge lined with jewellery shops. Free to cross; best early morning or evening to avoid crowds.
- **Piazza della Signoria and Palazzo Vecchio** — Open-air sculpture gallery and the old seat of government. The tower climb offers views; check current ticket prices on site.
- **Oltrarno and Piazzale Michelangelo** — Cross the river for Santo Spirito, artisan streets and the Boboli Gardens / Palazzo Pitti. Walk or bus up to Piazzale Michelangelo for the classic sunset panorama over the city and dome.

The historic centre is compact; most major sights lie within a 15–20 minute walk of each other. Trams and buses reach outer areas and the airport.

## Food & Local Cuisine

Tuscan cooking is robust and seasonal, built around high-quality ingredients rather than heavy sauces.

- **Bistecca alla fiorentina** — Thick T-bone steak, grilled over wood coals and served rare. Best shared and ordered by weight.
- **Ribollita** — Hearty vegetable and bread soup, a classic winter dish.
- **Other local plates** — Pappardelle with wild boar ragù, lampredotto (tripe sandwich from street stalls), pecorino cheese and Chianti Classico or Super Tuscan wines.
- **Markets** — Mercato Centrale (San Lorenzo) for lunch counters upstairs and produce downstairs; Sant’Ambrogio market in Santa Croce for a more local feel.
- **Gelato** — Seek places that list natural flavours and use seasonal fruit; avoid the brightest, most touristy displays.

Coperto (cover charge) is common; tipping is modest (round up or leave 5–10 % for good service). Reservations help at popular trattorias.

## Where to Stay

- **Historic centre (Duomo / San Lorenzo / Santa Croce)** — Most convenient for first visits; everything is on foot. Can be noisy and crowded by day.
- **Oltrarno** — Across the river; quieter evenings, artisan atmosphere, excellent restaurants. A short walk or bridge crossing from the main sights.
- **San Marco / near the Accademia** — Slightly calmer residential feel with easy access to the north side of the centre and the tram to the airport.

Book well ahead for spring, autumn and major events. Many properties are in historic buildings with character but limited lifts.

## Trip Budget

Approximate **per person, per day**, excluding international flights (2026 figures; prices vary by season and booking timing):

- **Budget:** €70–€120 — hostel or simple room, market or casual meals, free cathedral entry and walking.
- **Mid-range:** €150–€250 — mid-range hotel or apartment, restaurant meals, one major museum (Uffizi or Accademia) plus Duomo pass, some taxis or trams.
- **Comfort:** €300+ — boutique hotel, guided experiences, fine dining and wine.

Contactless cards are widely accepted. The city is best explored on foot; public transport is useful for the airport, Piazzale Michelangelo or day trips.

## Practical Tips

- Book timed tickets for the dome, Uffizi and Accademia weeks ahead in high season. Afternoon Uffizi slots are cheaper and quieter.
- The historic centre is a Limited Traffic Zone (ZTL); most visitors do not need a car.
- Pickpockets operate around the Duomo, Ponte Vecchio and Santa Maria Novella station; keep bags in front.
- Dress codes apply in churches (covered shoulders and knees).
- English is widely spoken in tourist areas; a few Italian phrases are appreciated.
- Weather is changeable; summer is hot and humid, winter can be cool and damp. Shoulder seasons are ideal.

## Conclusion

Florence packs an extraordinary concentration of Renaissance art and architecture into a walkable centre framed by the Arno and surrounding hills. Prioritise the Duomo complex (especially the dome climb), one major gallery, a river crossing into Oltrarno and a sunset view from Piazzale Michelangelo. With careful ticket planning and a relaxed pace, the city delivers both iconic sights and the quieter pleasures of Tuscan food and neighbourhood streets.
